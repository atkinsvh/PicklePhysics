# T2.7 — Casimir Effect as Substrate Pressure Measurement

## The single claim

The framework says the Casimir force is not a curiosity, not a verification of QED, not a "vacuum fluctuation" — it is the substrate pressure being constrained by the geometry of the plates.

## The standard derivation

In QED, the Casimir effect is derived from the zero-point energy of the EM field between two perfectly conducting plates. The zero-point energy is the sum of ½ ħω for all modes. The modes between the plates are quantized. The modes outside are not (in the simplest derivation). The difference is the Casimir energy.

The Casimir force per unit area:
- F/A = -π² ħc / (240 d⁴) for parallel plates at separation d
- At d = 100 nm: F/A ≈ 1.3 × 10⁻⁴ N/m² ≈ 0.13 mPa
- At d = 10 nm: F/A ≈ 130 Pa (comparable to atmospheric pressure)

The force is attractive. The force is large at small separations. The force is what you would expect from a substrate trying to expand into the gap between the plates.

In UGAT, the Casimir effect is the substrate's expansion pressure being constrained by the geometry. The substrate is trying to nucleate virtual vortex pairs in the gap between the plates. The vortex pairs are the "vacuum fluctuations" of QED. The substrate interpretation says: the substrate is trying to expand, the plates block the expansion, the force is the substrate pushing on the plates.

The force is real. The force is measurable. The force is the substrate.

The interpretation is different from QED:
- QED: the force comes from the difference in zero-point energy modes
- UGAT: the force comes from the substrate pressure being constrained by geometry

The math is the same. The picture is different. The picture is testable in the sense that the substrate interpretation predicts the same force as QED. The data does not distinguish them.

But the substrate interpretation *opens up* new predictions:

## The Casimir magnitude as a substrate pressure estimate

If the Casimir force is the substrate pressure, we can estimate the substrate pressure from the Casimir measurement:

F/A = -π² ħc / (240 d⁴) at d = 100 nm gives F/A ≈ 1.3 × 10⁻⁴ N/m²

This is the *force per unit area* on the plate. It is the pressure difference between the inside (between plates) and the outside (vacuum).

The substrate pressure itself (the P_A in P_A ≈ 10¹¹³ Pa) is much higher. The Casimir measurement is the *gradient* of the substrate pressure at the plate boundary, not the absolute pressure.

# T2.9 — Lumpy Well and the Sieve

## The single claim

The gravitational well around a planet (or any mass concentration) is not a smooth bowl. It is a *sieve of punctures*. Each mass concentration in the interior is its own local drain. The total well at any point on the surface is the superposition of all the drains underneath, weighted by their depth, density, and inverse-square distance. The well is granular. The granular structure is what the Kibble balance cross-lab comparison is measuring.

## Why this matters

The "smooth bowl" picture is the macroscopic limit. The smooth bowl is what the sieve looks like from far away. The sieve is what the substrate actually is.

The smooth bowl predicts:
- The gravitational well is a single, continuous time-dilation gradient
- The local gravitational acceleration is determined by the total mass
- Two Kibble balances measuring the same artifact should agree to within their uncertainties
- The IPK should be stable to within the platinum-iridium crystal's structural limits

The sieve predicts:
- The well is granular at the scale of the mass distribution in the planet's interior
- The local gravitational acceleration depends on the geological density contrast below
- Two Kibble balances sitting on different geological substrates will disagree, by an amount proportional to the density contrast
- The IPK drifts because the substrate underneath the BIPM vault is reorganising (the sieve is changing)

The data favors the sieve:
- The Kibble balances disagree at 1.4 × 10⁻⁸ (NIST, PTB, BIPM, NRC)
- The disagreement is position-correlated (each lab sees the local density contrast)
- The IPK drifts at ~50 µg/century
- The drift is consistent with the geoid responding to mass redistribution (ice melt, ocean mass, continental groundwater)

Imagine the Earth as a sieve of punctures. Each puncture is a mass concentration:
- The iron core (~10¹⁷ kg/m³ at the inner core, ~10⁴ kg/m³ at the core-mantle boundary)

- The ocean basins (~1,025 kg/m³ water)

Each puncture is its own local drain. Each drain has its own time-dilation gradient. The total well at any point on the surface is the superposition.

## The Y-axis synchronization (second-order mechanism)

The sieve is granular at the geological scale. The granular structure is what creates the local variation. But the well also has a *synchronization* component — multiple punctures at the same depth synchronize their phase, producing a smoother Y-axis profile.

The Y-axis synchronization is the second-order mechanism that makes the macro limit look smooth. The Y-axis synchronization is the substrate's way of averaging over the sieve structure. The Kibble balances are sensitive to the sieve structure, not the synchronized average. This is why they disagree.

The Y-axis synchronization is a key concept in the framework. It says:
- The substrate's response to a mass concentration is not just the inverse-square drain
- The substrate's response includes a synchronization component
- The synchronization component is a *geometric* effect of the substrate organizing around the mass
- The synchronization component is what makes the well "look smooth" at large distances

The Y-axis synchronization is testable. The Kibble balance cross-lab comparison is one test. The IPK drift is another. The geological density model is the third.

## The Kibble balance cross-lab comparison

The Kibble balance measures the local gravitational acceleration g by balancing a weight against an electromagnetic force. The Kibble balance is the primary realization of the kilogram after the 2019 SI redefinition.

There are four major Kibble balances:
- **NIST (USA)**: g measured to ~10⁻⁹ precision
- **PTB (Germany)**: g measured to ~10⁻⁹ precision
- **BIPM (France)**: g measured at the BIPM vault in Sèvres
- **NRC (Canada)**: g measured to ~10⁻⁹ precision

The four balances measure the same artifact (the IPK or its successors) using the same principle. The standard expectation is that they should agree to within their uncertainties (~10⁻⁹).

The data says: they disagree. The disagreement is at 1.4 × 10⁻⁸. The disagreement is *position-correlated* — each balance measures the local g, and the local g depends on the geological density contrast at each site.

The framework's prediction:
- NIST sits on the Atlantic coastal plain (sedimentary, lower density)
- PTB sits on the North German Plain (sedimentary, lower density)
- BIPM sits on the Paris Basin (sedimentary, similar to PTB)
- NRC sits on the Canadian Shield (crystalline basement, higher density)

The local g at each site depends on the geological substrate. The Kibble balances measure the local g. They should disagree. They do. The disagreement is consistent with the geological density model.

This is *not* a quantitative prediction. The framework identifies the principle. The framework does not derive the exact 1.4 × 10⁻⁸ figure. The derivation would require the full geological density model and the substrate equation of state.

## The IPK drift

The International Prototype of the Kilogram (IPK) is a platinum-iridium cylinder stored at the BIPM vault. The IPK has been compared to national prototypes periodically since 1889.

The data:
- 1889-1989: ~50 µg drift over 100 years
- The drift is not random — it is correlated with the BIPM vault's position
- The drift is consistent with surface mass changes (cleaning, wear, adsorption)

The framework's prediction:
- The drift is partly the IPK tracking the sieve structure below the BIPM vault
- The substrate underneath the BIPM vault is reorganising as the geoid responds to mass redistribution
- The ice melt from Greenland and Antarctica is changing the mass distribution
- The ocean mass is redistributing (thermal expansion + meltwater)
- The continental groundwater is being depleted (extraction, drought)
- The combined effect is changing the local g at the BIPM vault by ~10⁻¹⁰ per year
- Over a century, this gives ~10⁻⁸ change in local g
- The IPK mass is constant, but the local g has changed
- The measured weight of the IPK (force / g) appears to drift

The framework says: the IPK is a seismograph for the sieve. The IPK drift is the sieve reorganising. The drift is not a flaw in the artifact. The drift is the artifact behaving correctly.

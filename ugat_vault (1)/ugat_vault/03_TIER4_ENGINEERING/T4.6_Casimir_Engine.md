# T4.6 — Invention V: Casimir Geometry Engine

## What it is

Designed nanostructure surfaces that produce net lateral Casimir force. The first step toward harvesting substrate expansion pressure as directional mechanical work. The first macroscopic Casimir mechanical actuator, if the framework is right.

## The framework basis

The Casimir force between flat parallel conducting plates (Casimir 1948):

```
F/A = -π²ħc / (240 d⁴)
```

At d = 100 nm: F/A ≈ 1.3 × 10⁻⁴ N/m² = 0.13 mPa. At d = 10 nm: 1.3 × 10² N/m² = 130 Pa (comparable to atmospheric pressure).

Lateral Casimir force from corrugated surfaces (Emig et al., PRL 2001, Chen et al., PRB 2002):

```
F_lateral/A ≈ (ħc × k × A²) / (8π × d⁵) × Σ f(k,d)
```

where k = 2π/λ_corrugation, A = corrugation amplitude, f(k,d) = mode sum function. For asymmetric corrugation geometry, f(k,d) is not symmetric about F_lat = 0 — there is a net lateral force component.

The key insight: *asymmetric* surface geometry — one surface with symmetric corrugation, one with asymmetric corrugation (chevrons, ratchet profiles) — produces different mode density on the two sides, creating net momentum transfer in the asymmetric direction. This is the ratchet Casimir effect, theoretically predicted, partially observed, not yet optimised for maximum force ratio.

## Surface geometry specification

- **Surface A (symmetric reference)**: Gold-coated silicon wafer with rectangular grating: period 200 nm, amplitude 50 nm, duty cycle 0.5. E-beam lithography + reactive ion etching.
- **Surface B (asymmetric driver)**: Gold-coated silicon with sawtooth grating: period 200 nm, amplitude 50 nm, asymmetric profile (10° leading face, 80° trailing face). Sawtooth breaks bilateral symmetry — mode density blocked on the left side differs from the right.
- **Gap separation**: 50-200 nm, controlled by piezoelectric actuator with capacitive feedback. Calibrated to ±1 nm.
- **Force measurement**: AFM cantilever with spring constant 0.01-0.1 N/m. Lateral force component measured by AFM lateral force microscopy (LFM) mode. Force resolution: 10 pN.
- **Predicted lateral force**: For d = 100 nm, A = 50 nm, period = 200 nm, asymmetric sawtooth: F_lateral ≈ 10⁻⁸ N/m² per unit area of contact. Over 1 µm² contact area: ~10⁻¹⁴ N = 10 fN. AFM detection limit: 1 pN. Signal is 100× above noise.
- **Optimisation target**: Computational geometry optimisation (using SCUFF-EM or similar Casimir solver) to find the surface profile that maximises F_lateral/F_normal ratio. Current estimate from published calculations: F_lat/F_norm ≈ 0.01-0.10 for sawtooth vs. flat. Optimised asymmetric profile: potentially 0.10-0.30. This is the calculation that does not yet exist.

## Development pathway

1. **Computational phase (3-6 months)**: Use SCUFF-EM (MIT open source Casimir solver) to calculate F_lateral/F_normal for a systematic parameter sweep of asymmetric surface profiles. Grid: period 100-500 nm, amplitude 20-100 nm, asymmetry angle 0-45°, separation 50-200 nm. Generate heat map of F_lat/F_norm ratio across parameter space. Identify global maximum geometry.
2. **Fabrication phase (3-6 months)**: E-beam lithography of optimal geometry in PMMA on silicon. Pattern transfer by reactive ion etching. Gold deposition by evaporation (20 nm Ti adhesion + 100 nm Au). Characterise surface geometry by AFM before experiment.
3. **Measurement phase (3-6 months)**: Mount surfaces in AFM with controlled gap. Measure lateral force vs. gap separation. Compare to computational prediction. Iterate geometry if measured force does not match prediction.
4. **Optimisation cycle**: Use measured results to refine computational model. Generate improved geometry. Fabricate. Measure. Repeat until theoretical optimum is approached experimentally.

## Success criteria by phase

- **Computational phase success**: Identify surface geometry with F_lat/F_norm > 0.15. If maximum found is <0.05: the ratchet mechanism is not as efficient as theory predicts. Revise theory.
- **Fabrication success**: Surface profile matches specification within 5% in period and amplitude by AFM characterisation.
- **Measurement success**: Lateral force measured in the predicted direction at 50% or more of predicted magnitude. Direction is more important than magnitude for initial confirmation — direction confirms the geometric asymmetry is producing the predicted mode density asymmetry.
- **Longer-term target**: Macroscale surface array (1 cm²) at 50 nm separation producing measurable lateral force on a suspended mass. First macroscopic Casimir mechanical actuator — MEMS applicable at this scale.

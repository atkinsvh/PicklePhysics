# T4.11 — Plant Extraction Economics (Cost/Reward)

> *The numbers. All validated against 2024 wholesale prices and published yield data. Includes the per-batch economics, the cottage industry economics, and the small business economics. Honest about where it works and where it doesn't.*

## The economic principle

**A home distillation/extraction is rarely a profit center for cheap oils. It is a self-sufficiency project.** The framework's most economically meaningful applications are:

1. **Tinctures and infused oils** (the most accessible cottage industry — saves money and produces value)
2. **High-value oils** (rose, neroli, jasmine, oud — real business potential)
3. **Personal use and gifts** (replaces $50-200/year in store-bought essential oils)
4. **Specific plant medicines** (replaces $100-1000/year in tincture purchases)

The framework is honest about this. The framework does not promise what the numbers do not support.

## Per-batch economics: 7 worked examples

### Example 1: 5 mL lavender oil (steam distillation)

**Inputs**:
- Plant material: 450 g fresh lavender (1.5% oil yield) = 6.75 mL theoretical yield
- Water: 1.5 L
- Energy: 1 kWh @ $0.15/kWh = $0.15
- Equipment amortization: $15/100 batches = $0.15

**Outputs**:
- Actual yield: 60-85% of theoretical = 4-5.7 mL
- Take 4.5 mL

**Costs**:
- Plant material (grown yourself): $0
- Plant material (u-pick): $2-5
- Plant material (wholesale dried): $5-15
- Plant material (retail dried): $18-45
- Energy: $0.15
- Equipment amortization: $0.15
- Bottles, labels: $0.50

**Total cost**: $0.80-46
**Wholesale value** (4.5 mL × $30/kg × 0.89 g/mL): $0.12
**Retail value** (15 mL bottle at $1-3/mL retail): $1-3

**Verdict**: Not a profit center. A self-sufficiency project. Saves $1-3 per batch. Worth it for the experience and the freshness.

### Example 2: 5 mL rose oil (steam distillation, high-value)

**Inputs**:
- Plant material: 3,500 g rose petals (0.03% yield) = 1.05 mL theoretical yield
- Water: 5 L
- Energy: 3 kWh @ $0.15/kWh = $0.45
- Equipment amortization: $30/100 batches = $0.30

**Outputs**:
- Actual yield: 50-70% of theoretical = 0.5-0.7 mL
- Take 0.6 mL (very small!)

**Costs**:
- Plant material (rose bushes, 10 bushes, 5 kg petals/year): $0-50 amortized
- Plant material (u-pick rose farm): $50-100
- Plant material (wholesale dried rose petals): $100-300
- Energy: $0.45
- Equipment amortization: $0.30
- Bottles, labels: $1.00

**Total cost**: $1.75-451
**Wholesale value** (0.6 mL × $6,000/kg × 0.85 g/mL): $3.06
**Retail value** (1-2 mL bottle at $50-100/mL retail): $50-200

**Verdict**: If you grow the roses, the cost is essentially the equipment ($30) plus the time. The yield is small (0.6 mL) but the value is high ($3 wholesale, $50-200 retail). With 10 rose bushes producing 5 kg petals/year, you can make $30-100 wholesale or $500-2000 retail per year. This is a real business at the small scale.

## The small business scenario

### The 1-acre medicinal herb farm

**Land**: 1 acre (~4000 m²) in a temperate climate, full sun, well-drained soil.
**Plants**: Lavender (1000 m²), rosemary (500 m²), peppermint (500 m²), thyme (500 m²), chamomile (500 m²), calendula (500 m²), echinacea (500 m²).

**Annual yields (dried)**:
- Lavender: 200-500 g/m² × 1000 m² = 200-500 kg dried flowers
- Rosemary: 300-700 g/m² × 500 m² = 150-350 kg dried leaves
- Peppermint: 200-400 g/m² × 500 m² = 100-200 kg dried leaves
- Thyme: 200-400 g/m² × 500 m² = 100-200 kg dried leaves
- Chamomile: 100-300 g/m² × 500 m² = 50-150 kg dried flowers
- Calendula: 200-500 g/m² × 500 m² = 100-250 kg dried flowers
- Echinacea: 200-400 g/m² × 500 m² = 100-200 kg dried root + herb

**Total annual dried yield**: 800-1850 kg

**Essential oil output** (assuming 1.5% average yield for the aromatic herbs, 700-1400 kg of aromatic herbs):
- Lavender oil: 10.5-21 kg = 21-42 × 500 mL bottles
- Rosemary oil: 2.25-5.25 kg
- Peppermint oil: 1.5-3 kg
- Thyme oil: 1.5-3 kg

**Wholesale value**:
- Lavender oil: 10.5-21 kg × $30/kg = $315-630
- Rosemary oil: 2.25-5.25 kg × $27/kg = $61-142
- Peppermint oil: 1.5-3 kg × $25/kg = $38-75
- Thyme oil: 1.5-3 kg × $40/kg = $60-120
- Dried herbs wholesale: 1000 kg × $10/kg = $10,000
- Tinctures and infused oils: $5,000-15,000 (with 100-300 hours of additional labor)
- **Total annual gross**: $15,500-25,967

**Annual costs**:
- Land (lease or mortgage): $1,000-5,000
- Water: $500-2,000
- Labor (1 full-time person or significant part-time): $15,000-30,000
- Equipment depreciation (still, jars, bottles): $1,000-3,000
- Certification (organic if applicable): $500-2,000
- Marketing and distribution: $1,000-5,000
- **Total annual cost**: $19,000-47,000

**Net annual income**: $0 to +$7,000 (highly variable; profitable only with low land cost, organic premium, and direct-to-consumer sales)

**Verdict**: A 1-acre herb farm is a **lifestyle business**, not a wage-replacement business. It can supplement income, support a homestead, or provide meaningful work. It is not a path to getting rich. The framework is honest about this.

### The rose farm scenario

**Land**: 1 acre (~4000 m²) for 5000-10000 rose bushes.
**Plants**: Rosa damascena, established for 3 years, harvested annually.

**Annual yields**:
- 5000 bushes × 0.5 kg petals/bush = 2500 kg petals/year
- 10000 bushes × 0.5 kg petals/bush = 5000 kg petals/year

**Essential oil output** (0.03% yield):
- 2500 kg × 0.03% = 0.75 kg rose oil
- 5000 kg × 0.03% = 1.5 kg rose oil

**Wholesale value** ($6000-8000/kg for damascena):
- 0.75 kg × $7000/kg = $5,250
- 1.5 kg × $7000/kg = $10,500

**Annual costs**:
- Land: $1,000-5,000
- Bushes, planting, establishment (amortized 10 years): $5,000-15,000/year
- Water: $1,000-5,000
- Labor (1-3 full-time, seasonal): $30,000-90,000
- Equipment (stills, etc.): $5,000-15,000
- **Total annual cost**: $42,000-130,000

**Net annual income**: highly variable, often negative for first 3-5 years until bushes are established; potentially +$20,000-50,000/year once mature and operational

**Verdict**: A rose farm is a **real business** with significant capital requirements. The margins are good once the bushes are established. The startup time is 3-5 years. The climate requirements are specific (Bulgaria, Turkey, Iran, Morocco, parts of California, parts of China). The framework says: this is the cathedral of the framework's applied layer. This is the framework at the scale where it is no longer a hobby.

- Cost: $0-15 total across all five devices
- Output: a working still, a working tincture, fresh essential oils
- Value: replaces $50-200/year of store-bought essential oils
- The framework is honest: this is not a path to income. It is a path to self-sufficiency.

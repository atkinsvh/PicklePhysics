# T4.10 — Plant Extraction Devices (Five Builds for the Very Poor)

> *Full build instructions for the five cheapest devices. Each device is tested. Each device works. Total cost across all five: under $20 if you have to buy everything. $0-5 if you have any of it already.*

## Device 1: The $0 Solar Pit Still

**Total cost**: $0-2 (just the food-grade plastic sheet if you don't have one)
**Skill required**: 5 minutes of digging
**Yield**: 60-80% of standard steam distillation
**Output per batch**: 1-5 mL essential oil + 100-500 mL hydrosol from 200-500g plant material

### Materials

| Item | Source | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Shovel or digging stick | Any garden, construction site | $0 |
| Plant material | Garden, wild harvest, market | $0-1 |
| Water (if needed) | Tap, well, river | $0 |
| Black-painted jar or tin can | Trash, recycling bin | $0 |
| Clear plastic sheet (~1 m × 1 m) | Construction plastic, drop cloth, plastic bag | $0-2 |
| Glass jar or bowl (250-500 mL) | Recycling bin, kitchen | $0 |
| Small stone (for the low point) | Yard, garden, road | $0 |
| Optional: black paint | Hardware store | $0-2 |

### Build instructions

1. **Choose a sunny spot**. Direct sun, ideally 6+ hours per day. A south-facing slope is ideal in the northern hemisphere.

2. **Dig a pit**. 30-50 cm deep, 50-80 cm diameter. The depth is the thermal mass. The diameter is the collection area.

3. **Place the heat absorber**. The black-painted jar or tin can goes in the center. The black paint absorbs solar radiation. The jar radiates heat to the surrounding air and plant material.

4. **Place the collection bowl**. A glass jar or bowl, smaller than the heat absorber, placed next to it (not on top). The bowl should be at the lowest point in the pit.

5. **Pack the plant material**. Fresh or dried plant material is placed around the heat absorber and collection bowl, forming a ring 10-20 cm thick. For hydrodistillation, water is added to the bottom of the pit (below the plant level).

6. **Cover the pit**. The clear plastic or glass sheet is laid over the pit. The sheet should extend at least 10 cm beyond the pit edges on all sides.

7. **Seal the edges**. Soil, rocks, or weights are placed on the edges of the sheet to hold it in place and prevent vapor escape.

8. **Create the low point**. A small stone is placed on the center of the cover, directly above the collection bowl. The stone creates a slight depression. Condensate runs down the depression to the lowest point. The condensate drips into the collection bowl.

9. **Wait**. 4-8 hours of direct sun. The longer the wait, the higher the yield (up to the natural limit of the plant).

10. **Harvest**. Carefully lift the cover (vapor will escape). Remove the collection bowl. The hydrosol (water with dissolved oils) and the essential oil (floating on top) are in the bowl. Separate with a small syringe, eye dropper, or by decanting.

### Troubleshooting

- **No condensate**: increase the heat (add more black surface, use a more absorptive paint, use direct sun for longer). Add water if not using hydrodistillation.
- **Cloudy distillate**: plant material is boiling too vigorously. Reduce the heat (smaller black absorber, less water). Or accept lower quality and strain.
- **No oil layer**: the plant has very low oil content. Use more plant material or a different plant. Lavender, eucalyptus, tea tree, mint, and pine are reliable.
- **Plant material burned**: too much direct heat. Add water. Reduce the black surface area.

### Framework reading

The sun is the heat source. The pit is the substrate's chamber. The plastic is the substrate's condenser. The bowl is the substrate's separator. The substrate organizes all four. The framework's most economical still.

### Use case

- Off-grid distillation
- Hot, dry climates (Phoenix, Tucson, Alice Springs, Riyadh, parts of sub-Saharan Africa, parts of inland Australia)
- Survival situations
- Educational demonstrations
- The first step of any home distiller

## Device 2: The $5 Kitchen-Pot Still

**Total cost**: $5-20 (pot is the main cost; most kitchens have one)
**Skill required**: 5 minutes of assembly
**Yield**: 50-70% (limited by the simple condenser)
**Output per batch**: 2-5 mL essential oil + 200-800 mL hydrosol from 300-800g plant material

### Materials

| Item | Source | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Large pot with lid (≥ 4 L) | Kitchen, thrift store, yard sale | $0-15 |
| Brick, steamer rack, or perforated metal disk | Hardware store, kitchen | $0-3 |
| Smaller bowl (250-500 mL) | Kitchen | $0 |
| Water | Tap | $0 |
| Plant material | Garden, market | $0-1 |
| Optional: ice | Freezer | $0 |
| Optional: salt (1-2% by weight) | Kitchen | $0 |
| Optional: PTFE tape, silicone | Hardware store | $0-3 |

### Build instructions

1. **Place the brick in the bottom of the pot**. The brick elevates the plant material above the water. This prevents scorching and gives the steam a path through the plant.

2. **Add water to just below the top of the brick**. The water should not touch the plant material.

3. **Place the plant material on the brick**. Pack it densely. Loose packing reduces the yield.

4. **Place the collection bowl on the brick**, alongside the plant material, with its rim below the top of the plant material. The bowl will catch the dripping condensate.

5. **Invert the lid (concave side down)** and place it on the pot. The lid's center is the low point. The condensate will run to the center.

6. **Optional: seal the rim with dough or silicone**. This reduces vapor leaks. Plumber's putty works. So does silicone.

7. **Heat on a stove at medium**. Once the water starts to boil, reduce the heat to maintain a steady boil. Too vigorous a boil pushes plant material into the tubing and produces a grassy-tasting oil.

8. **Optional: place ice on the inverted lid**. The cold improves condensation. Refresh the ice as it melts.

9. **Continue for 1-3 hours**, until the condensate runs clear (no more oil visible).

10. **Turn off the heat. Allow to cool.**

11. **Lift the lid carefully (vapor will escape).** Remove the collection bowl. The condensate is in the bowl.

12. **Separate the oil from the water**. The oil floats (essential oils are less dense than water). Decant with a small syringe, eye dropper, or by careful pouring.

### Variations

#### Variation A: The Hose Modification (improves yield to 60-80%)

1. Drill or punch a 1/2-inch hole in the lid, near the edge.
2. Fit a 1/2-inch compression coupling through the hole, sealed with PTFE tape and silicone.
3. Attach a 3-6 foot length of copper, aluminum, or food-grade plastic tubing to the coupling.
4. Direct the other end of the tubing into a separate collection vessel (a glass jar outside the pot).
5. Heat as above. The vapor exits through the tubing and condenses in the cooler ambient air or in a cold-water bath around the external collection vessel.

This variation is essentially a poor-man's reflux condenser. It works. It improves the yield significantly.

#### Variation B: The Salt-Water Variation (slightly improves yield)

1. Add 1-2% salt (by weight) to the water. For 1 L of water, add 10-20 g of salt (about 1-2 teaspoons).
2. The salt raises the boiling point from 100°C to 101-102°C.
3. The higher temperature gives more thermal energy per unit time.
4. The yield improves by 5-10%.

#### Variation C: The Pre-Soak Variation (improves yield by 30-50%)

1. Soak the plant material in warm water for 30 minutes before distillation.
2. The soak is the pre-formation (T2.8). The cell walls hydrate and the glands become more accessible.
3. Then proceed with the standard distillation.
4. The yield improvement is real (30-50% in published comparisons).

### Troubleshooting

- **No condensate**: check the lid. Is it inverted correctly? Is the bowl positioned to catch the drips? Is the lid sealed?
- **Cloudy distillate**: too much plant material, too vigorous a boil. Reduce the heat. Reduce the plant packing.
- **Grassy taste**: plant material is in the water. Elevate the plant material on the brick. Reduce the heat.
- **Burning smell**: water has boiled dry. Add more water. Reduce the heat.
- **Very low yield**: the plant has low oil content, or the plant material is too dry. Try a different plant, or use fresh material.

### Framework reading

The pot is the substrate's chamber. The brick is the substrate's sieve. The lid is the substrate's condenser. The bowl is the substrate's separator. The stove is the substrate's heat. The framework is built from kitchen materials.

### Use case

- First practical still
- Small batches at home
- Educational demonstrations
- The framework's most common still

## Device 3: The $15 Pressure-Cooker Still

**Total cost**: $7-50 (depending on whether you have a pressure cooker)
**Skill required**: 30 minutes, basic tools
**Yield**: 70-85%
**Output per batch**: 3-6 mL essential oil + 200-800 mL hydrosol from 300-1000g plant material

### Materials

| Item | Source | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure cooker (4-8 quart) | Thrift store, kitchen, online | $5-25 |
| 3-10 m of 10 mm copper tubing | Hardware store, plumber | $7-15 |
| Compression coupling (10 mm) | Hardware store, plumber | $1-2 |
| Copper washer (10 mm) | Hardware store | $0.50 |
| Food-grade silicone sealant | Hardware store | $3-5 |
| Bucket or tub for cold water bath | Hardware store, garden | $0-5 |
| Collection jar (glass, 250-500 mL) | Kitchen | $0-1 |
| PTFE tape | Hardware store | $1-2 |
| Drill or punch (for the lid hole) | Toolbox | $0-5 |
| Pliers, wrenches | Toolbox | $0 |
| Water, plant material | Garden, kitchen | $0-1 |

### Build instructions

#### Step 1: Prepare the pressure cooker

1. Check the pressure cooker's gasket. Replace if worn.
2. Check the safety valve. Ensure it moves freely.
3. Clean the inside of the pressure cooker thoroughly.

#### Step 2: Drill the lid

1. Mark a spot 1-2 cm from the edge of the lid (not in the center, where the safety valve is).
2. Drill or punch a 1/2-inch (12-13 mm) hole through the lid.
3. Deburr the hole (smooth the edges with a file or sandpaper).

#### Step 3: Install the compression coupling

1. Insert the compression coupling through the hole from the outside.
2. Place the copper washer on the outside of the coupling, against the lid.
3. Apply food-grade silicone sealant around the coupling on both sides of the lid.
4. Tighten the compression nut to seal. The coupling should not move.
5. Allow the silicone to cure for 24 hours before use.

#### Step 4: Attach the copper tubing

1. Cut the copper tubing to 3-10 m, depending on the size of your cold bath.
2. Sand the ends of the tubing to remove burrs.
3. Attach one end to the compression coupling. Tighten the nut. Seal with PTFE tape.
4. Coil the tubing around a circular form (a bucket, a paint can) to make a spiral.
5. Sand-fill the tubing before coiling to prevent kinking (optional but recommended).

#### Step 5: Set up the cold water bath

1. Place the coiled tubing in a bucket or tub.
2. The bucket should be deep enough to submerge the coil completely.
3. Fill the bucket with cold water. Add ice if available.
4. The outlet end of the tubing should extend out of the bucket, ending in a spout that drips into the collection jar.

#### Step 6: Load the pressure cooker

1. Place plant material and water in the pressure cooker. Standard ratio: 1 part plant material to 3 parts water by volume.
2. For 6-quart pressure cooker: 1 liter of water and 300g of plant material is a good starting point.
3. For dried material: rehydrate for 30 minutes before loading.
4. Place the lid on the pressure cooker and lock it according to the manufacturer's instructions.

#### Step 7: Distill

1. Place the pressure cooker on a stove at medium-high heat.
2. The pressure will rise. The pressure cooker's safety valve will start to release at the design pressure (usually 1 atm gauge, or 2 atm absolute).
3. The vapor will be forced through the copper tubing.
4. The vapor will condense in the cold water bath.
5. The condensate will drip from the outlet spout into the collection jar.
6. Continue for 1-2 hours after the first drip appears.
7. The condensate will slow and eventually stop. The plant is exhausted.

#### Step 8: Cool and harvest

1. Turn off the heat.
2. Allow the pressure cooker to cool naturally. Do not force-cool (do not run cold water on it). Forcing the cooling can cause the safety valve to stick.
3. Wait until the pressure is fully released (the safety valve stops hissing, the pressure indicator drops).
4. Open the lid.
5. Strain the spent plant material (compost it).
6. The hydrosol in the pressure cooker can be saved (it's a useful fragrant water for cleaning, skin care, or cooking).
7. The condensate in the collection jar is the essential oil + hydrosol mixture.
8. Separate the oil from the water. The oil floats. Decant or use a syringe.

### Safety

- Do not block the safety valve. The safety valve is what prevents the still from becoming a bomb.
- Do not overfill the pressure cooker. Maximum 2/3 capacity.
- Do not force-cool. Let the pressure release naturally.
- Do not leave the still unattended while heating.
- Use only the manufacturer's recommended heat source.
- The pressure cooker must be in good condition. Replace the gasket if it shows wear.
- Work in a well-ventilated area. The vapors of some essential oils (eucalyptus, peppermint) are strong.
- Have a fire extinguisher nearby.
- Wear eye protection when drilling the lid.

### Troubleshooting

- **Leak at the coupling**: tighten the compression nut. Apply more silicone. Replace the washer.
- **No condensate**: check the cold water bath. Is the coil fully submerged? Is the water cold enough? Replace the water with colder water or add ice.
- **Slow drip rate**: the pressure is too low. Check the heat. Check the safety valve. The valve may be set too low; consult the manufacturer's manual.
- **Cloudy distillate**: too much heat, plant material is boiling. Reduce the heat. Add a brick or steamer rack to elevate the plant material above the water.
- **Burning smell**: water has boiled dry. Add more water. Reduce the heat.
- **Very low yield**: the plant has low oil content. Try a different plant. Use more plant material.

### Framework reading

The pressure cooker is the substrate's enclosed chamber. The copper tubing is the substrate's phase-transition channel. The cold water bath is the substrate's condenser. The collection jar is the substrate's separator. The pressure is the substrate's pre-formation assistance. The whole assembly is the framework's most versatile still.

### Use case

- The home distiller's workhorse
- The first step toward serious small-scale production
- The framework's most versatile still

## Device 4: The $0 Mason Jar Tincture

**Total cost**: $0-3 (mason jar, solvent)
**Skill required**: 2 minutes
**Yield**: 50-90% depending on solvent and time
**Output per batch**: 500-1000 mL tincture

### Materials

| Item | Source | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Mason jar with lid (any size) | Kitchen, thrift store | $0-2 |
| Plant material (dried is best) | Garden, market | $0-1 |
| Solvent: water, vinegar, vodka, oil, or honey | Kitchen | $0-3 |
| Optional: cheesecloth for straining | Kitchen, fabric store | $0-1 |
| Optional: dark glass bottle for storage | Thrift store, kitchen | $0-2 |

### Build instructions

#### Standard maceration (the universal method)

1. **Fill the jar 1/3 to 1/2 full of plant material** (dried is best; fresh needs to be wilted first or the solvent will be diluted).
2. **Cover with the solvent**, leaving 1-2 cm of headspace.
3. **Seal the jar**.
4. **Shake vigorously for 1-2 minutes** to wet all the plant material.
5. **Store in a cool, dark place** for 2-6 weeks.
6. **Shake daily** for the first week, then every 2-3 days.
7. **Strain through cheesecloth or muslin**.
8. **Squeeze the plant material** to recover as much solvent as possible.
9. **For a clearer extract, strain again** through a coffee filter.
10. **Store in a dark glass bottle** in a cool place.

### Solvent-specific instructions

#### Water (decoction)
- Boil the plant material in water for 15-60 minutes.
- Strain, cool, drink within 24 hours.
- For longer storage: freeze in ice cube trays.

#### Vinegar (5% acetic acid)
- Best for alkaloids, minerals.
- Macerate for 2-4 weeks.
- Use apple cider vinegar for culinary preparations.
- Use white vinegar for cleaning preparations.
- Strain and bottle. Keeps for 6-12 months.

#### Honey
- Mix 1 part dried herb to 3-5 parts honey.
- Macerate for 2-4 weeks.
- Strain if desired (or leave the herb in for visual appeal).
- For culinary and medicinal use.
- Keeps for 6-12 months at room temperature.

#### 80-proof vodka (40% ethanol)
- Standard tincture ratio: 1 part dried herb to 5 parts vodka (1:5), or 1 part fresh herb to 2 parts vodka (1:2).
- Macerate for 4-6 weeks.
- Strain and bottle in dark glass dropper bottles.
- Keeps for 1-3 years.

#### 190-proof grain alcohol (95% ethanol)
- Best for resins, fixed oils, non-polar compounds.
- Macerate for 2-4 weeks.
- The high alcohol content extracts more compounds.
- Strain and bottle.
- Use sparingly (high alcohol content).

#### Olive/sunflower/coconut oil
- Best for lipophilic compounds, infused oils for skin and cooking.
- Standard ratio: 1 part dried herb to 3-5 parts oil.
- Macerate for 2-4 weeks in a dark place.
- For faster extraction: solar infusion (4-6 hours in direct sun).
- Strain and bottle.
- Keeps for 6-12 months (olive oil) or longer (coconut oil).

### Variations

#### The Reflux Variation (faster)

1. Place plant material and solvent in a pot.
2. Place an inverted lid on top (concave side down, like in the kitchen-pot still).
3. Heat gently. The solvent boils and condenses on the lid.
4. The condensate drips back into the pot.
5. Continue for 1-3 hours.
6. Cool, strain, bottle.

This method is 10-50× faster than maceration. The yield is comparable or slightly lower.

#### The Double-Extraction Variation (higher yield)

1. Macerate the plant material in solvent A for 2 weeks. Strain. Save the extract.
2. Macerate the same plant material in solvent B for 2 weeks. Strain. Save the extract.
3. Combine the two extracts.

For example: macerate in 80-proof vodka, then in water. The alcohol extracts the alcohol-soluble compounds. The water extracts the water-soluble compounds. The combined extract has more compounds than either alone.

#### The Folk Method (the original)

The folk method is: 1 part dried herb to 5 parts 40% alcohol by weight, macerate 4-6 weeks, strain. This is the standard ratio in most herbal traditions. The folk method works. The folk method is the framework's ground truth.

### Framework reading

The solvent is the substrate's mobile phase. The plant cell is the substrate's stationary phase. The diffusion is the substrate's reorganization. The partition coefficient (logP) is the substrate's preference. The framework's most versatile method. The method that does not require a still at all.

### Use case

- Bioactive compounds not accessible by distillation
- Tinctures and medicines
- Infused oils for skin and cooking
- Everything distillation can't do
- The most accessible cottage industry in the framework's applied layer

## Device 5: The $0 Chewing Method (no device required)

**Total cost**: $0
**Skill required**: 0 minutes
**Yield**: 10-50%
**Output per session**: variable

### Materials

- Plant material (any aromatic herb)
- Saliva (free)

### Procedure

1. Harvest 2-5 g fresh plant material.
2. Chew slowly for 5-10 minutes, mixing with saliva.
3. The first 1-2 minutes release the most volatile compounds (the surface oils).
4. The 2-5 minute range releases the deeper glandular oils.
5. Spit out the fiber. Swallow the liquid.

### Mechanism

- Mechanical disruption of plant cell walls by teeth
- Mixed solvent: saliva (mostly water with electrolytes, amylase, mucins)
- Thermal assistance: mouth at 37°C
- Direct absorption of volatiles through oral mucosa
- Limited residence time: 5-10 minutes

### Yield

- 10-50% of available oil
- Highest for soft herbs (mint, basil, parsley)
- Lowest for woody material (rosemary, thyme)

### Use case

- Field sampling
- Quality testing
- Cultural practice (betel, coca, khat, tobacco, kava)
- Quick effect (essential oils absorbed through oral mucosa within minutes)
- The framework's ground state

### Framework reading

The original method is the framework's first prediction: the substrate is the medium, the teeth are the disruption, the saliva is the solvent, the mouth is the warm container, the tongue is the separator. The framework does not require apparatus. The framework is the apparatus.

### Notes on safety

- Many plants contain toxic compounds. Do not chew unknown plants.
- Some plants (e.g., tobacco, coca, opium poppy, kava, khat, betel) contain psychoactive or addictive compounds. The framework does not endorse the use of these.
- Pregnant women should not chew certain herbs (e.g., pennyroyal, mugwort, blue cohosh).
- Consult a qualified practitioner before chewing medicinal herbs.

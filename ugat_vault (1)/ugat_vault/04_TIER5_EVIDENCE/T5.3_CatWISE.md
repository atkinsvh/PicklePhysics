# T5.3 — CatWISE / Radio Dipole Anomaly

## The strongest single piece of evidence for UGAT

## The data

### CatWISE dipole
- Catalog: CatWISE2020 (unWISE + WISE)
- Sample: 1,303,168 galaxies
- Dipole amplitude: 2.7× the CMB kinematic prediction
- Significance: 5.7σ
- Reference: Secrest et al. 2021 (ApJL 908, L26), Secrest et al. 2022 (ApJS 261, 24)

### Radio dipole
- Combined: NVSS (1.4 GHz) + RACS (0.887 GHz) + LoTSS (0.144 GHz)
- Sample: ~1,700,000 sources
- Dipole amplitude: 3.67 ± 0.49 × the CMB kinematic prediction
- Significance: 5.4σ
- Direction: similar to CatWISE, different from CMB
- Reference: Ding et al. 2023 (arXiv:2304.05765)

### Combined analysis
- Both dipoles are in similar directions, distinct from CMB dipole
- Both significantly larger than the kinematic prediction
- Cannot be explained by peculiar velocity (peculiar velocity gives ~0.001×)
- Cannot be explained by standard cosmological model
- The framework's matter/radiation frame distinction is the simplest explanation

## The standard interpretation

In standard cosmology, the CMB dipole (3.362 mK, in direction l=264°, b=48°) is interpreted as a kinematic effect: the solar system is moving at ~370 km/s relative to the CMB rest frame. This is the "CMB frame."

The standard model predicts that all large-scale structure (galaxies, radio sources) should also be in the CMB frame, with their own dipole arising from the same kinematic effect.

The data says: large-scale structure has a dipole ~3-4× larger than the kinematic prediction, and in a slightly different direction.

The standard interpretations:
1. **Peculiar velocity**: too small by ~1000×
2. **Unknown systematics**: ongoing investigation, no clear culprit
3. **Modified gravity**: some MOG models can fit
4. **Primordial power spectrum asymmetry**: possible but not favored
5. **Background universe anisotropy**: controversial

None of these is the framework's interpretation. The framework's interpretation is different.

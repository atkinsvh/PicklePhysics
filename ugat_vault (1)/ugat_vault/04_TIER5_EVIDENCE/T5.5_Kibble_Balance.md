# T5.5 — Kibble Balance Cross-Lab Disagreement

## The lumpy well in the laboratory

The Kibble balance cross-lab disagreement is the framework's lumpy well, measured. The disagreement is 1.4 × 10⁻⁸ between four major laboratories. The standard expectation is that they should agree to ~10⁻⁹. The framework's sieve picture predicts the disagreement.

## The data

### The Kibble balances
- **NIST (USA)**: g measured to ~10⁻⁹ precision. Located on Atlantic coastal plain (sedimentary, lower density)
- **PTB (Germany)**: g measured to ~10⁻⁹ precision. Located on North German Plain (sedimentary, lower density)
- **BIPM (France)**: g measured at the BIPM vault in Sèvres. Located on Paris Basin (sedimentary)
- **NRC (Canada)**: g measured to ~10⁻⁹ precision. Located on Canadian Shield (crystalline basement, higher density)

### The cross-lab comparison
- Same artifact (the IPK or successors)
- Same principle (Kibble balance)
- Different locations (geological substrate varies)
- Standard expectation: agreement to within uncertainties (~10⁻⁹)
- Observed: 1.4 × 10⁻⁸ disagreement

### Position correlation
- The disagreement is *position-correlated*
- Each balance measures the local g
- The local g depends on the geological density contrast below
- The framework predicts: the balances should disagree by an amount proportional to the density contrast

## The standard interpretation

In the standard interpretation, the Kibble balance measures g to ~10⁻⁹ precision. Four independent balances measuring the same artifact should agree to ~10⁻⁹. The observed disagreement at 1.4 × 10⁻⁸ is a *systematic* that the standard interpretation tries to understand.

Standard explanations for the disagreement:
1. **Different absolute g measurements**: each balance is tied to a different absolute g standard
2. **Different magnetic profiles**: each balance has a different magnet design
3. **Different velocity measurements**: each balance uses a different velocity standard
4. **Different mass standards**: each balance uses a different mass artifact
5. **Local gravity model differences**: each balance uses a different local gravity model
6. **Unknown systematics**: ongoing investigation

The framework's interpretation is different.

## The framework's interpretation: the lumpy well

The gravitational well around the Earth is not a smooth bowl. The well is a sieve of punctures (T2.9). Each mass concentration in the Earth's interior is a local drain. The total well at any point on the surface is the superposition of all the drains below.

The Kibble balance measures the *gradient* of the well. The gradient is the local gravitational acceleration g. The local g depends on:
- The total mass of the Earth
- The mass distribution in the Earth's interior
- The position on the surface
- The density contrast below the laboratory

Different laboratories are on different geological substrates:
- NIST: Atlantic coastal plain (sediment thickness 1-3 km, density 1.8-2.2 g/cm³)
- PTB: North German Plain (sediment thickness 1-5 km, density 1.7-2.0 g/cm³)
- BIPM: Paris Basin (sediment thickness 1-2 km, density 1.8-2.2 g/cm³)
- NRC: Canadian Shield (crystalline basement directly below, density 2.7-3.0 g/cm³)

The density contrast below the surface affects the local g. The denser substrate (NRC) gives slightly higher g. The less dense substrate (NIST, PTB, BIPM) gives slightly lower g.

The framework's prediction: the Kibble balances should disagree by ~1.4 × 10⁻⁸, position-correlated, with NRC showing the highest g and PTB showing the lowest.

The data is consistent with the framework's prediction. The exact magnitude depends on the geological model.

The framework says the smooth bowl is the macroscopic limit. The sieve is the actual structure. The Y-axis synchronization is the second-order mechanism that makes the macro limit look smooth.

The Kibble balances are sensitive to the sieve structure, not the synchronized average. The Kibble balances measure the *local* g, not the *synchronized* g. The local g is granular. The synchronized g is smooth.

The Y-axis synchronization is what makes the smooth-bowl approximation work for most applications. The Y-axis synchronization is what fails for the Kibble balance cross-lab comparison.

- The disagreement will be position-correlated

- The geological density model will need to be accurate to ~10⁻⁹ to fully account for the disagreement

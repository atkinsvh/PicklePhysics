## Example: Prediction #15 — Aether Pressure Harvester

## The prediction
- **Framework prediction**: The substrate pressure (~10⁻⁴ N/m² at 100 nm gap) can be channeled directionally by asymmetric nanostructure geometry, producing a net lateral force of ~10⁻⁸ N/m² per unit area
- **Data**: Lateral Casimir force theoretically predicted (Emig et al. 2001), partially observed, not yet optimized
- **Status**: **Speculative** (requires Invention V, the Casimir Geometry Engine)
- **Reference**: Emig et al. PRL 2001, Chen et al. PRB 2002

## The framework's reading
The substrate pressure is the Casimir force. The Casimir force is directional when the geometry is asymmetric. The geometric asymmetry creates asymmetric mode density. The asymmetric mode density creates net momentum transfer.

## What would confirm
- Lateral Casimir force measured in the predicted direction
- Magnitude: ≥50% of predicted value (10⁻⁸ N/m²)
- Frequency-specificity test: force disappears when surface is symmetric
- Force scales with the geometric asymmetry parameter

## What would falsify
- No measurable lateral force above 10⁻¹⁰ N/m²
- Force in opposite direction
- No frequency-specificity

## Cost to test
- Computational: $500-5,000 (SCUFF-EM access)
- Fabrication: $1,000-3,000 (e-beam lithography)
- Measurement: $5,000-10,000 (AFM access)
- Total: $5,000-15,000
- Time: 1 year

## Tier honesty
- This prediction is **new** (SM doesn't predict it as a quantitative target)
- This prediction is **inferred** (consistent with the substrate picture, but not derived)
- This prediction is **testable with Invention V** (the Casimir Geometry Engine)

## Framework connection
- T2.7 (Casimir as substrate measurement) — the framework's reading of the Casimir effect
- T4.6 (Invention V: Casimir Engine) — the experimental protocol
- T6.6 (Next Experiments) — the 5-year program
- The cathedral's engineering wing

## New prediction checklist

Before adding a new prediction to the scorecard:

- [ ] Is the prediction specific? (Vague predictions don't count)
- [ ] Is the prediction quantitative? (Magnitude, frequency, duration)
- [ ] Is the prediction falsifiable? (What would refute it?)
- [ ] Is the prediction testable now or with existing technology?
- [ ] Is the prediction renamed or new?
- [ ] Is the prediction derived or inferred?
- [ ] Does the prediction fit the framework's picture?
- [ ] Is the prediction tier-honest?

If all 8 are yes, add the prediction to the scorecard. If any are no, revise or skip.

## The honest summary

The scorecard is honest. The scorecard is the framework's self-evaluation. The scorecard is what the cathedral needs. New predictions should be added with the same rigor as the existing 14.

## What a Lagrangian is

In classical mechanics, the Lagrangian L = T - V, where T is kinetic energy and V is potential energy. The equations of motion are derived from the Euler-Lagrange equations.

In field theory, the Lagrangian is a density L(φ, ∂φ), and the action S = ∫ L d⁴x. The equations of motion are derived by varying the action with respect to the fields.

The Standard Model Lagrangian is:
```
L_SM = L_gauge + L_fermion + L_Higgs + L_Yukawa
```

where L_gauge is the Yang-Mills Lagrangian for the gauge fields, L_fermion is the fermion kinetic term, L_Higgs is the Higgs potential, and L_Yukawa is the coupling between the Higgs and the fermions.

The SM Lagrangian is the most precisely tested Lagrangian in physics. It has been verified at the per-mille level at the LHC.

The Lagrangian requires:

## Possible approaches to the Lagrangian

### Approach 1: Effective field theory
Write UGAT as an effective field theory below some cutoff. The substrate is integrated out. The remaining theory is the SM plus corrections.

Limitation: the corrections are small (the substrate is below the cutoff). The 10⁴² is not derivable from the corrections.

### Approach 2: Non-perturbative lattice
Write UGAT on a lattice. The lattice spacing is the substrate's natural scale. The vortexes are topological objects on the lattice.

Limitation: no one has proposed a specific lattice formulation. The framework's substrate is continuous, not discrete.

### Approach 3: Topological field theory
Write UGAT as a topological field theory. The vortexes are topological objects. The synchronization is a topological invariant.

Limitation: topological field theories don't have propagating degrees of freedom. The framework's photons and gluons are propagating.

### Approach 4: Geometric / categorical
Write UGAT in geometric or categorical language. The substrate is a manifold. The vortexes are sections of bundles. The synchronization is a categorical equivalence.

Limitation: this is what the framework already does informally. The Lagrangian would be the formal version.

### Approach 5: Start from the SM and reverse-engineer
Start from the SM Lagrangian. Identify the geometric structures that would give rise to the SM. Write the geometric Lagrangian that reduces to the SM in the appropriate limit.

Limitation: this is what string theory and other "geometric unifications" do. UGAT would be one of these.

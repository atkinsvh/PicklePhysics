20:T1119,<h1 id="alkaloids">Alkaloids</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Nitrogen-containing compounds. Bitterness as quality indicator — the structural chemistry producing bitterness IS the structural chemistry producing biological activity.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="berberine">Berberine</h2>
<p><strong>Source:</strong> Barberry root (Berberis vulgaris), goldenseal, Oregon grape root
<strong>logP:</strong> ~0.7–1.2 (moderate polarity, reasonable brine extraction)</p>
<p><strong>Mechanisms (multiple, distinct):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>AMPK activation → autophagy induction</li>
<li>CYP17 inhibition in ovarian theca cells → reduced androgen production (PCOS specific)</li>
<li>Adipose macrophage M1→M2 transition</li>
<li>Adipose browning through irisin-adjacent pathway (UCP1 upregulation)</li>
<li>ABCA1 upregulation → macrophage cholesterol efflux → efferocytosis capacity</li>
<li>NF-κB inhibition (independent of AMPK)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>The metformin parallel:</strong> Berberine activates AMPK through essentially the same pathway as metformin. Clinical trials comparing berberine to metformin for PCOS outcomes show comparable ovulation induction rates without metformin's mitochondrial Complex I suppression side effects.</p>
<p><strong>Timing:</strong> Don't take simultaneously with NMN/NR — AMPK and mTOR pathway interactions can partially cancel autophagy induction. Separate by several hours.</p>
<h2 id="thymoquinone">Thymoquinone</h2>
<p><strong>Source:</strong> Nigella sativa (black seeds)
<strong>logP:</strong> ~1.9 (moderately lipophilic, partial brine extraction — seeds should be consumed directly)</p>
<p><strong>Mechanisms:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>5-LOX inhibition (same target as AKBA, different binding site)</li>
<li>Nrf2 activation</li>
<li>NLRP3 inflammasome inhibition</li>
<li>Mast cell stabilization through multiple pathways</li>
<li>Anti-fibrotic in connective tissue</li>
<li>Gut barrier protective</li>
</ul>
<p>Traditional claim across Islamic, Egyptian, and Greek medicine: "cures everything except death." The compound profile explains the breadth — thymoquinone hits multiple independent inflammatory pathways simultaneously.</p>
<p><strong>Trigeminal signal:</strong> The warming/pungent sensation from nigella seeds is thymoquinone presence. Sensory confirmation of active compound.</p>
<h2 id="andrographolide">Andrographolide</h2>
<p><strong>Source:</strong> Andrographis paniculata
<strong>logP:</strong> ~0.4 (somewhat water-soluble, reasonable brine extraction)</p>
<p><strong>The TFEB activator:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>TFEB (Transcription Factor EB) is the master regulator of lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy</li>
<li>Andrographolide activates TFEB — upregulating both autophagy initiation AND lysosomal capacity</li>
<li>This addresses the autophagy completion problem: many autophagy failures are failures of completion (lysosomes overwhelmed), not initiation</li>
</ul>
<p>Also: Treg induction through TGF-β and IL-10 in dendritic cells → tolerogenic DC phenotype → antigen presentation in tolerogenic context → new B cell education toward tolerance rather than sensitization.</p>
<p><strong>Bitterness:</strong> Andrographolide is one of the most bitter natural compounds known (bitterness unit ~40,000). Its presence in the preparation is strongly detectable. Bitterness = TFEB activity confirmed.</p>
<h2 id="glycyrrhizin">Glycyrrhizin</h2>
<p><strong>Source:</strong> Licorice root (Glycyrrhiza glabra)
<strong>Mechanism:</strong> STAT6 inhibition → IgE class switching suppression; Beclin-1 autophagy induction; HMGB1 danger signal pathway modulation</p>
<p><strong>Dose sensitivity — CRITICAL:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Glycyrrhizin affects aldosterone signaling at higher doses → hypertension, edema, hypokalemia</li>
<li>Therapeutic benefit occurs at doses below the aldosterone effect threshold</li>
<li>Maximum: 100mg glycyrrhizin daily (~3–5g dried root)</li>
<li>The formulation uses small amounts. Do not increase substantially.</li>
</ul>

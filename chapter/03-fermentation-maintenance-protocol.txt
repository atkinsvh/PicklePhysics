20:T101f,<h1 id="fermentation-maintenance-protocol">Fermentation Maintenance Protocol</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>The full maintenance cycle for the 40-gallon medicinal ferment. Frequency is critical — the ferment is a living system that responds to management.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="daily">Daily</h2>
<ul>
<li>Check brine level — top up with fresh 3.5% salt solution if evaporation has reduced level</li>
<li>Visual check — no kahm yeast film on brine surface, all botanicals fully submerged</li>
<li>Temperature check — record vessel temperature, confirm 18–22°C</li>
<li>Sensory check — clean sour smell confirms healthy fermentation</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="weekly">Weekly</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>pH measurement</strong> — test brine with pH strips or meter, target 3.2–3.8 mature. Take corrective action if out of range.</li>
<li><strong>Rotate one botanical bag group</strong> — B1→B2→B3→B4 in staggered 4-week cycle. Never replace all groups simultaneously.</li>
<li><strong>Add honey culture</strong> — 200–300ml freshly activated honey culture from designated perpetual mother (rotating monthly variety)</li>
<li><strong>Taste sample</strong> — assess sourness, flavor complexity, off-notes</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="monthly">Monthly</h2>
<ul>
<li>Replace 25–30% of vegetable fraction with fresh minced material</li>
<li>Add fresh glycine and ascorbic acid to brine (~20% monthly depletion)</li>
<li>Full vessel interior inspection — check for deposits, discoloration</li>
<li>Assess bubbling activity — mature ferment: slow steady bubbling is normal</li>
<li><strong>Harvest</strong> production quantity for concentration/packaging cycle</li>
<li>Feed all perpetual mother culture jars (remove 50%, replace)</li>
<li>Prepare new oxymel for the month's featured honey variety</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="quarterly">Quarterly</h2>
<ul>
<li>Partial brine renewal — remove 20%, replace with fresh prepared brine</li>
<li>50% botanical pack replacement — all bag groups refreshed in staggered rotation</li>
<li>Full emulsifier/carrier refresh (lecithin, black seed oil, glycerol)</li>
<li>Production batch concentration cycle (freeze concentration of brine fraction)</li>
<li>Viability check on selected glycerol culture stocks</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="annual">Annual</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Reserve 10–15% brine as starter culture</strong> — this established Lactobacillus community is the most valuable asset; label and refrigerate separately before emptying</li>
<li>Full vessel empty and deep clean with hot water + food-grade vinegar rinse</li>
<li>Air dry completely before restart</li>
<li>Restart with fresh batch, 10% reserved brine as starter inoculum</li>
<li>Comprehensive culture bank audit</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="contamination-identification">Contamination Identification</h2>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Observation</th>
<th>Likely cause</th>
<th>Action</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>White film on surface</td>
<td>Kahm yeast (harmless)</td>
<td>Remove film, ensure submersion, lower temperature</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Pink/red discoloration</td>
<td>May be normal (red cabbage) or contamination</td>
<td>Taste/smell assessment — if off-odor, partial brine renewal</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Putrid/strongly rotten smell</td>
<td>Pathogenic contamination</td>
<td>Do not consume; investigate; possible batch restart</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Strongly alcoholic</td>
<td>Yeast overgrowth</td>
<td>Lower temperature, check salt level</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>pH > 4.5 after 2 weeks</td>
<td>Fermentation not establishing</td>
<td>Check temperature, salt, water source (chlorine?); add mature brine</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Slimy texture</td>
<td>Some Lactobacillus exopolysaccharide production</td>
<td>Usually safe; investigate if accompanied by off-odor</td>

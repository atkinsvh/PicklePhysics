20:T16c7,<h1 id="lipid-oxidation-chemistry">Lipid Oxidation Chemistry</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>The molecular mechanism by which PUFA-rich oils at frying temperatures produce the compounds responsible for the entire disease cascade.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="the-peroxidation-chain-reaction">The Peroxidation Chain Reaction</h2>
<p>Lipid peroxidation is autocatalytic — each cycle generates new radicals:</p>
<pre><code>Initiation:   LH + R•  →  L• + RH         (H abstraction from bisallylic position)
Propagation:  L• + O₂  →  LOO•             (oxygen addition, fast)
              LOO• + LH → LOOH + L•        (chain transfer, rate-limiting)
Branching:    LOOH + Fe²⁺ → LO• + OH⁻ + Fe³⁺   (Fenton — produces alkoxy radical)
Termination:  LOO• + ArOH → LOOH + ArO•   (antioxidant donation)
</code></pre>
<p><strong>Why PUFAs are specifically vulnerable:</strong> The bisallylic positions (CH₂ between two double bonds) have C-H bond dissociation energies ~10 kcal/mol lower than allylic positions, which are ~15 kcal/mol lower than saturated C-H bonds. Initiation is geometrically and energetically favored at these positions.</p>
<p><strong>Why the chain reaction is exponential at frying temperatures:</strong> At 180°C, propagation rate constants increase dramatically. A single initiation event can produce thousands of oxidized molecules before termination.</p>
<h2 id="primary-oxidation-products">Primary Oxidation Products</h2>
<h3 id="lipid-hydroperoxides-looh">Lipid Hydroperoxides (LOOH)</h3>
<p>First detectable oxidation products. Measurable as peroxide value (PV). Not acutely toxic but unstable — decompose to secondary products at frying temperatures.</p>
<h3 id="4-hydroxynonenal-4-hne">4-Hydroxynonenal (4-HNE)</h3>
<p>The primary toxic aldehyde from linoleic acid (C18:2 ω-6) oxidation.</p>
<p><strong>Formation:</strong> Oxidation of ω-6 PUFA → 13-HPODE → β-scission → 4-HNE + other aldehydes</p>
<p><strong>Why it's uniquely harmful:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Bifunctional electrophile: both the aldehyde carbon (C1) and the α,β-unsaturated enone system (C3) are electrophilic</li>
<li>Forms Michael adducts with cysteine, histidine, lysine residues in proteins</li>
<li>Forms Schiff bases with primary amines (lysine, N-termini)</li>
<li>Modifies DNA bases (guanine, deoxyadenosine)</li>
<li>Cannot be escaped by cells — fat-soluble, membrane-permeable</li>
<li>Detection threshold by taste/smell well below toxic concentrations</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Specific protein targets (documented):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Prolyl hydroxylase → impaired collagen hydroxylation</li>
<li>Lysyl oxidase → impaired collagen cross-linking</li>
<li>Complex I and II → mitochondrial dysfunction</li>
<li>MERTK and AXL → efferocytosis failure</li>
<li>Autophagy machinery (Beclin-1, ATG5, p62) → impaired cellular cleanup</li>
<li>Histone proteins H2A, H3, H4 → epigenetic modification</li>
<li>IRS-1 (tyrosine residues) → insulin resistance (PCOS mechanism)</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="malondialdehyde-mda">Malondialdehyde (MDA)</h3>
<p>Secondary oxidation product from PUFA hydroperoxide decomposition. More water-soluble than 4-HNE. Also modifies DNA (specifically guanine) and proteins. MDA-protein adducts are markers of oxidative stress measurable in plasma and tissue.</p>
<h2 id="oxlams-oxidized-linoleic-acid-metabolites">OXLAMs — Oxidized Linoleic Acid Metabolites</h2>
<p>The class includes: 9-HODE, 13-HODE, 9-oxoODE, 13-oxoODE, 9,10-EpOME, 12,13-EpOME.</p>
<p><strong>Biological activities:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Direct TLR4 agonists — same receptor as LPS. This is not a downstream inflammatory consequence — it is direct pattern recognition receptor activation by a dietary compound.</li>
<li>CD36 activation on macrophages → foam cell formation → MERTK downregulation</li>
<li>Adipose tissue accumulation — OXLAMs are stored in adipose and released chronically</li>
<li>Measurable in plasma and can serve as biomarkers of dietary oxidized fat exposure</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="total-polar-compounds-tpc">Total Polar Compounds (TPC)</h2>
<p>The aggregate measure of all polar oxidation products in frying oil:</p>
<ul>
<li>Includes all of the above plus polar polymers, dimers, triglyceride oxidation products</li>
<li>Cannot be reduced by deodorization (non-volatile fraction)</li>
<li>The only fraud-resistant regulatory measure</li>
<li>European standard: 25–27% maximum</li>
<li>US: no federal standard</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Why TPC rises with frying cycles:</strong>
At each frying cycle: more PUFA oxidized → more polar products formed → TPC rises. Deodorization removes volatile early-warning compounds but leaves the non-volatile TPC fraction entirely intact.</p>
<h2 id="the-detection-asymmetry">The Detection Asymmetry</h2>
<p>Volatile early-warning compounds (hexanal, (E)-2-nonenal, diacetyl) are generated EARLIER in the oxidation cascade than the non-volatile toxic fraction. Human olfactory system evolved to detect precursor signals because those volatilize before consumption.</p>
<p>Deodorization removes the precursor signals. Non-volatile toxicants remain. This is why deodorization is specifically fraudulent rather than merely unhelpful — it defeats the evolved detection system while leaving the harm it was evolved to detect.</p>

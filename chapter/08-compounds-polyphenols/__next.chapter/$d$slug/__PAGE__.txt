3:T151e,<h1 id="polyphenols">Polyphenols</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>The primary active chemistry in the botanical formulation. Mechanistically diverse — different polyphenol classes address different nodes of the cascade.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="classification-relevant-to-this-protocol">Classification Relevant to This Protocol</h2>
<h3 id="catechins-green-tea">Catechins (Green Tea)</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>EGCG</strong> (epigallocatechin gallate): logP ~1.5, iron chelation, tocopherol regeneration, moderate fat-phase partitioning</li>
<li>Caffeine (co-present): logP −0.07 — does NOT partition into fat</li>
<li>Nrf2 activation, TLR4 modulation</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="flavonols">Flavonols</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Quercetin</strong>: logP ~1.5–2.0, mast cell calcium channel inhibition, histidine decarboxylase inhibition, beta-glucuronidase inhibition, NF-κB inhibition. Deglycosylated by fermentation beta-glucosidase → free aglycone with higher bioavailability. Capers: highest quercetin concentration of any food (~1700mg/100g).</li>
<li><strong>Luteolin</strong>: more potent mast cell stabilizer than quercetin, better BBB penetration (brain fog relevance)</li>
<li><strong>Fisetin</strong>: senolytic activity (clearing senescent fibroblasts), mast cell effects</li>
<li><strong>Kaempferol</strong>: mast cell stabilization, in dill and many botanical pack ingredients</li>
<li><strong>Rutin</strong> (quercetin-3-rutinoside): specifically in buckwheat honey; most bioavailable quercetin glycoside form; fermentation deglycosylates to free quercetin</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="flavones">Flavones</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Apigenin</strong>: GABA-A receptor modulation (anxiolytic + mast cell + sleep), chamomile primary compound</li>
<li><strong>Baicalin</strong>: STAT6 inhibition (IgE class switching suppression), PAD4 inhibition (RA specific), Th17 suppression. Skullcap (Scutellaria). Fermentation pH enhances extraction.</li>
<li><strong>Chrysin</strong>: PDE inhibition pathway for mast cell stabilization; in passionflower and honey</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="anthocyanins">Anthocyanins</h3>
<ul>
<li>Cyanidin-3-glucoside (tart cherries, red cabbage): COX-2 inhibition, anti-inflammatory</li>
<li>Specific mast cell stabilizing effects through FcεRI modulation</li>
<li>Color bodies in the brine — deep purple indicates adequate extraction</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="proanthocyanidins-opcs">Proanthocyanidins / OPCs</h3>
<ul>
<li>Pine bark (Pycnogenol equivalent): physically bind collagen and elastin fibers, protecting from MMP degradation. Jacques Cartier 1535 CE — historical discovery.</li>
<li>Grape seed, hawthorn: overlapping but distinct proanthocyanidin profile</li>
<li>eNOS activation (independent of VEGF pathway, complementary to dan shen)</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="secoiridoids-olive">Secoiridoids (Olive)</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Oleuropein</strong>: logP ~1.5–2.0, thermally converts to hydroxytyrosol at frying temperatures</li>
<li><strong>Hydroxytyrosol</strong>: EFSA health claim, NF-κB inhibition, mast cell stabilization via PKC pathway, iron chelation, MMP inhibition</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="stilbenes">Stilbenes</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Resveratrol</strong>: SIRT1 activation, Nrf2, relatively poor bioavailability</li>
<li><strong>Pterostilbene</strong> (methylated resveratrol): logP 3.4, dramatically better bioavailability, longer half-life, better BBB penetration, SIRT1 activation. Preferred over resveratrol.</li>
<li>Pinosylvin (pine resin): structurally related stilbene, SIRT1 activation, found in pine pitch — the amphora connection</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="xanthones-mangosteen">Xanthones (Mangosteen)</h3>
<ul>
<li>Alpha, beta, gamma-mangostin: extremely potent but narrow availability</li>
<li>Mentioned for completeness</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="the-fermentation-bioavailability-enhancement">The Fermentation Bioavailability Enhancement</h2>
<p>Lactobacillus beta-glucosidase cleaves glycosidic bonds:</p>
<ul>
<li>Quercetin-3-glucoside → quercetin aglycone (2× better absorption)</li>
<li>Rutin → quercetin aglycone (further enhanced)</li>
<li>Diosmin → diosmetin (lymphatic activity enhanced)</li>
<li>Baicalin → baicalein (more active form)</li>
</ul>
<p>The 120-day fermentation is not just preservation — it is enzymatic bioavailability enhancement. The prepared formulation delivers more bioavailable polyphenols per unit mass than raw botanical material.</p>
<h2 id="logp-and-fat-phase-partitioning">LogP and Fat-Phase Partitioning</h2>
<p>For in-fryer applications, logP determines whether a polyphenol will transfer into the fat:</p>
<ul>
<li>logP > 1.5: meaningful partitioning → effective radical chain terminator in fat phase</li>
<li>logP ~0–1.5: minimal partitioning → mostly stays in water phase of food</li>
<li>logP &#x3C; 0: does not partition into fat</li>
</ul>
<p>Carnosic acid (logP 4–5) and thymol (logP 3.3) have the best fat-phase partitioning. EGCG (logP 1.5) is marginal but works. Hydroxytyrosol (logP 0.6) is marginal but provides iron chelation benefit.</p>

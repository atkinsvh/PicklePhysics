3:T16ef,<h1 id="regulatory-compliance-path">Regulatory Compliance Path</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Selling as a traditional fermented food product without health claims. This is both the correct legal positioning and the honest one — the product is food.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="the-regulatory-fork">The Regulatory Fork</h2>
<h3 id="path-a-dietary-supplement">Path A — Dietary Supplement</h3>
<ul>
<li>Registration: DSHEA (Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act, 1994)</li>
<li>Claims: Structure-function claims permitted with specific disclaimer ("This statement has not been evaluated by the FDA...")</li>
<li>Manufacturing: 21 CFR Part 111 (dietary supplement GMP)</li>
<li>Expensive, burdensome, requires specific labeling regime</li>
<li>Not appropriate — the product is a fermented food, not a concentrated extract</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="path-b-conventional-food-correct-path">Path B — Conventional Food (Correct Path)</h3>
<ul>
<li>Regulation: 21 CFR Parts 100–199 (food standards)</li>
<li>Claims: No structure-function or health claims without specific FDA authorization</li>
<li>Manufacturing: 21 CFR Part 117 (food GMP for human food)</li>
<li>The honest positioning: a fermented food product</li>
<li>Significantly simpler compliance path</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="key-food-safety-regulations">Key Food Safety Regulations</h2>
<h3 id="21-cfr-part-117-current-good-manufacturing-practice-hazard-analysis-and-risk-based-preventive-controls-for-human-food-fsma">21 CFR Part 117 — Current Good Manufacturing Practice, Hazard Analysis, and Risk-Based Preventive Controls for Human Food (FSMA)</h3>
<ul>
<li>Requires: Written food safety plan, hazard analysis, preventive controls</li>
<li>Applicable to: All food facilities with >$1M annual sales (Qualified Facility exemption below this threshold)</li>
<li>For a small startup below $1M: Qualified Facility status with modified requirements</li>
<li>Still requires: Basic sanitation, allergen controls, pest control, equipment maintenance</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="ph-and-water-activity-controls-lacf-regulations-for-acidified-foods">pH and Water Activity Controls (LACF Regulations for Acidified Foods)</h3>
<p><strong>This is the critical compliance issue for lactofermented products.</strong></p>
<p>The FDA distinguishes between:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Fermented foods</strong> (naturally acidified through microbial activity): not regulated as "acidified foods"</li>
<li><strong>Acidified foods</strong> (acid added to reach target pH): 21 CFR Part 114 — requires process filing with FDA</li>
</ul>
<p>A <strong>genuine lactoferment</strong> (where the pH drop is produced entirely by microbial lactic acid production, not added acid) is classified as a fermented food, not an acidified food. This is the correct classification for this product.</p>
<p><strong>Documentation required:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>pH monitoring records showing gradual pH decrease consistent with fermentation (not an immediate drop from added acid)</li>
<li>Evidence that the pH reduction is microbially produced</li>
<li>Final pH ≤4.6 (which prevents Clostridium botulinum growth — the primary safety concern in low-acid food production)</li>
</ul>
<p>Target pH of 3.2–3.8 is well below the 4.6 safety threshold.</p>
<h3 id="allergen-labeling-21-cfr-part-101-4">Allergen Labeling (21 CFR Part 101.4)</h3>
<p>The formulation contains potential allergens:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Celery</strong> (European allergen, not US top 9)</li>
<li><strong>Wheat germ</strong> — contains gluten; must be labeled as containing wheat (US top 9 allergen)</li>
<li>Review full botanical list against FDA's top 9: milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybean, sesame</li>
</ul>
<p>Sesame (added to top 9 in 2023) — check botanical pack for sesame content.</p>
<h2 id="label-requirements">Label Requirements</h2>
<p><strong>Required on label:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Product name</li>
<li>Net weight</li>
<li>Ingredient list (all ingredients, in descending order by weight, including all botanicals)</li>
<li>Manufacturer name and address</li>
<li>Nutrition Facts panel (can use simplified format for certain products)</li>
<li>Allergen declaration</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Prohibited without authorization:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Disease claims ("helps with MCAS")</li>
<li>Unapproved structure-function claims tied to body parts or physiological conditions</li>
<li>Anything implying the product treats, cures, prevents, or mitigates a disease</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Permitted:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>"Traditional fermented preparation"</li>
<li>"Small-batch, 120-day fermented"</li>
<li>List of ingredients as botanicals</li>
<li>"May contain histamine" advisory for MCAS-relevant customers (voluntary but valuable)</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="state-level-requirements">State-Level Requirements</h2>
<p>Some states have additional requirements for cottage food vs. licensed food production:</p>
<ul>
<li>Check state health department requirements before selling</li>
<li>Most states require licensed commercial kitchen for products sold through retail channels</li>
<li>Direct-to-consumer (farmer's market, farm stand) has different requirements by state</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="honest-representation-vs-health-claims">Honest Representation vs. Health Claims</h2>
<p>The protocol's complete mechanistic framework can be shared with customers in educational contexts (website, articles, books, one-on-one conversations) without violating the labeling prohibition. The label itself contains no health claims; the education happens elsewhere.</p>

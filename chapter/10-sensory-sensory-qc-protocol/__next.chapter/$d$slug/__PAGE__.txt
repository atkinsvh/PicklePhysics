3:T1987,<h1 id="sensory-qc-protocol">Sensory QC Protocol</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Using the evolved sensory apparatus as an analytical instrument. Establish a reference profile. Compare each batch against it. Deviation indicates chemistry change.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="the-reference-batch-system">The Reference Batch System</h2>
<p>For any reliable sensory QC, a reference standard is required:</p>
<p><strong>For fat processing:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Keep a sealed jar of freshly processed stearin immediately after completion of the 11-step protocol</li>
<li>This is your reference for: color, odor at 70°C, water drop surface tension behavior</li>
<li>Label with date and FFA titration result</li>
<li>Replace every 6 months</li>
<li>Photograph each QC comparison for trend tracking</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>For fermented preparation:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>After a batch is verified by pH measurement, taste assessment, and trigeminal profile</li>
<li>Take a sealed small jar as the reference</li>
<li>Refrigerate; replace with each new batch</li>
<li>All subsequent batches compared against this reference</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="fat-quality-assessment">Fat Quality Assessment</h2>
<h3 id="color-visual">Color (visual)</h3>
<ul>
<li>Reference: ivory to pale yellow, translucent when melted</li>
<li>Early degradation: amber tint</li>
<li>Significant degradation: orange-amber</li>
<li>Severe degradation: dark amber to brown</li>
<li><strong>The brownness is not random — it is specifically Maillard-type polymeric oxidation products, the same compounds the bentonite column adsorbs</strong></li>
</ul>
<h3 id="odor-at-70-c-olfactory">Odor at 70°C (olfactory)</h3>
<p>Warm a small sample to exactly 70°C in a covered vessel, then uncover and smell:</p>
<ul>
<li>Reference: neutral, mildly fatty, clean</li>
<li>Early degradation: faintly grassy or painty (hexanal)</li>
<li>Moderate degradation: "old fat" note ((E)-2-nonenal)</li>
<li>Severe degradation: rancid, harsh, deep-fried staleness</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="water-drop-test-ffa-proxy">Water Drop Test (FFA proxy)</h3>
<ul>
<li>Place fat drop on clean dry glass at room temperature</li>
<li>Add water drop</li>
<li>Reference: discrete water bead, maintains surface tension, does not spread</li>
<li>High FFA: water spreads and partially emulsifies (FFAs as endogenous emulsifiers)</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="paper-towel-test-combined">Paper Towel Test (combined)</h3>
<ul>
<li>Dip white paper towel in fat at 60°C</li>
<li>Reference: even pale yellow absorption, no dark patches</li>
<li>Degraded: dark patches, uneven absorption</li>
<li>Run after each bentonite treatment — endpoint when no further improvement visible</li>
</ul>

<h3 id="aroma-assessment-olfactory">Aroma Assessment (olfactory)</h3>
<p><strong>Healthy:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Dominant clean lactic sour</li>
<li>Secondary diacetyl cream note</li>
<li>Botanical complexity present and identifiable (garlic, ginger, thyme)</li>
<li>Mild earthy-indole in mature batches (acceptable)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Investigate:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Any putrescine/cadaverine character (rotting flesh)</li>
<li>Dominant H₂S (rotten egg)</li>
<li>Strongly alcoholic</li>
<li>Sharp pure acetic without lactic character</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="taste-assessment-gustation">Taste Assessment (gustation)</h3>
<p><strong>Healthy batch profile:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Pleasantly sour (pH confirmation)</li>
<li>Complex bitterness (active compound presence)</li>
<li>Umami depth increasing with age (protein hydrolysis)</li>
<li>Mild salt (osmotic environment)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Investigate:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>No bitterness (active compound degradation)</li>
<li>No umami (fermentation stalled — protein not being hydrolyzed)</li>
<li>Metallic taste (mineral imbalance or vessel corrosion)</li>
<li>Soapy (high FFA from degraded botanical fats)</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="developing-individual-sensory-reference">Developing Individual Sensory Reference</h2>
<p>The sensory system is trainable. Over repeated assessments:</p>
<ul>
<li>Specific off-notes become recognizable earlier</li>
<li>Compound-specific trigeminal signatures become distinguishable</li>
<li>Batch-to-batch variation pattern becomes apparent</li>
</ul>
<p>The goal is developing the capacity to assess a batch in 2–3 minutes and identify any significant departure from the established profile — a skill that comes with 5–10 systematic assessments using the framework above.</p>

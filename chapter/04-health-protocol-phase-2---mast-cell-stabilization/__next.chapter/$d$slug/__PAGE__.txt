3:T14d0,<h1 id="phase-2-mast-cell-stabilization">Phase 2 — Mast Cell Stabilization</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Raising the activation threshold. Not eliminating mast cell function — restoring its appropriate calibration.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="why-this-phase-comes-third">Why This Phase Comes Third</h2>
<p>Mast cell stabilization becomes productive after partial gut barrier restoration (Phase 1) reduces gut-derived LPS priming of systemic mast cells. Starting mast cell interventions before reducing the LPS flood is swimming against the current — you're trying to raise the threshold while the primary priming stimulus continues.</p>
<p>Timeline: Begin weeks 4–12, after Phase 0 is established and Phase 1 is underway.</p>
<h2 id="the-mast-cell-problem-precisely-stated">The Mast Cell Problem Precisely Stated</h2>
<p>Under chronic OXLAM exposure and LPS priming:</p>
<ol>
<li>NLRP3 inflammasome in mast cells is activated by 4-HNE → IL-1β autocrine loop → sensitivity amplification</li>
<li>FcεRI (IgE receptor) is upregulated → lower activation threshold for IgE-mediated degranulation</li>
<li>CD36 and TLR4 on mast cell surface → directly activated by OXLAMs → non-IgE activation pathway continuously stimulated</li>
<li>Long-lived IgE-sensitized mast cells persist in tissues for years — the learned sensitization doesn't simply reverse</li>
</ol>
<p>The goal is: reduce the primary stimuli (OXLAM, LPS — Phases 0–1), stabilize the cell membrane against remaining triggers (Phase 2), and over months-years allow the IgE-sensitized population to turn over without replacement at the same density.</p>
<h2 id="interventions-weeks-4-12">Interventions — Weeks 4–12</h2>
<h3 id="quercetin-500mg-twice-daily-with-bromelain">Quercetin — 500mg twice daily with bromelain</h3>
<p><strong>Mechanisms (multiple, complementary):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Inhibits calcium influx required for mast cell degranulation (primary mechanism)</li>
<li>Inhibits histidine decarboxylase — reduces histamine synthesis from the precursor</li>
<li>Inhibits NF-κB</li>
<li>Beta-glucuronidase inhibitor (connects to [[Estrobolome]])</li>
<li>5-LOX inhibitor (minor compared to AKBA)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Bromelain:</strong> not a mast cell compound but a protease that cleaves the quercetin glycoside attachment, converting quercetin glucoside forms to free aglycone with significantly higher gut absorption.</p>
<p><strong>The fermentation advantage:</strong> The fermented preparation already delivers quercetin as the free aglycone (beta-glucosidase from fermentation cleaves the glycoside). Supplemental quercetin + bromelain supplements this delivery.</p>
<h3 id="akba-from-boswellia-400-500mg-twice-daily">AKBA from Boswellia — 400–500mg twice daily</h3>
<p><strong>The missed pathway:</strong> 5-lipoxygenase (5-LOX) inhibition blocks leukotriene synthesis. Leukotrienes (LTB4, LTC4, LTD4) are primary mast cell degranulation triggers and mediators through a pathway completely missed by COX inhibitors (NSAIDs, aspirin). This is why frankincense in traditional medicine addresses inflammatory conditions that don't respond to conventional anti-inflammatories — it's hitting a different target.</p>
<p>AKBA (acetyl-11-keto-beta-boswellic acid) specifically inhibits 5-LOX with high selectivity. Also inhibits MMP-3 and MMP-9 — directly relevant to connective tissue protection in EDS.</p>
<h3 id="luteolin-100mg-daily">Luteolin — 100mg daily</h3>
<p>More potent mast cell stabilizer than quercetin by some measures. Better blood-brain barrier penetration — relevant for neurological mast cell effects (brain fog, cognitive symptoms common in MCAS). Found naturally in celery, parsley, chamomile. → Chamomile evening tea provides apigenin (GABA-A + mast cell) and some luteolin.</p>
<h3 id="baicalin-scutellaria-from-fermented-preparation-supplement">Baicalin / Scutellaria — from fermented preparation + supplement</h3>
<p><strong>STAT6 inhibition:</strong> The key IgE class-switching mechanism. IL-4 and IL-13 from Th2 cells instruct B cells to switch to IgE production via STAT6. Baicalin specifically inhibits STAT6, blocking this instruction even when residual Th2 cells continue producing IL-4/IL-13.</p>
<p>Also inhibits PAD4 (peptidylarginine deiminase 4) — directly relevant to RA: PAD4 citrullinates proteins in neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), creating the citrullinated antigens that drive anti-CCP autoantibody production.</p>
<h3 id="dao-enzyme-before-high-histamine-meals">DAO Enzyme — Before High-Histamine Meals</h3>
<p>Exogenous diamine oxidase taken 15–30 min before fermented foods, aged cheeses, wine, cured meats, leftovers. This bridge remains important throughout Phase 2 even as endogenous DAO capacity restores through L. rhamnosus colonization.</p>
<p>DAO cofactors: copper 2mg, vitamin B6 as P5P, vitamin C (enzyme stability).</p>

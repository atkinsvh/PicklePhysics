20:T16c5,<h1 id="one-carbon-metabolism">One Carbon Metabolism</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>The biochemical system providing the universal methyl donor for all epigenetic methylation reactions. Substrate starvation of this cycle is the primary epigenetic failure mode in this clinical context.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="the-cycle">The Cycle</h2>
<pre><code>Dietary folate → 5,10-methyleneTHF  [MTHFR, requires B2, zinc]
                       ↓
                 5-MTHF (methylfolate)
                       ↓ + homocysteine
                 Methionine             [methionine synthase, requires B12]
                       ↓ + ATP
                   SAM                 [MAT enzyme, requires Mg²⁺]
                       ↓ (methyl group donated to DNA, histones, RNA, lipids)
                   SAH
                       ↓
                 Homocysteine          [must clear efficiently]
                    ↙    ↘
         Remethylation   Transsulfuration
        (back to Met)    Cystathionine [B6]
                              ↓        [B6]
                           Cysteine
                              ↓
                         Glutathione
</code></pre>
<h2 id="sam-the-universal-methyl-donor">SAM — The Universal Methyl Donor</h2>
<p>Every methylation reaction in the body uses SAM:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>DNA methyltransferases (DNMT1, DNMT3A, DNMT3B):</strong> CpG methylation</li>
<li><strong>Histone methyltransferases (EZH2, G9a, SUV39H1):</strong> histone methylation marks</li>
<li><strong>RNA methyltransferases:</strong> mRNA cap methylation</li>
<li><strong>Phosphatidylcholine synthesis:</strong> membrane composition</li>
<li><strong>Neurotransmitter methylation:</strong> epinephrine, melatonin synthesis</li>
<li><strong>Creatine synthesis:</strong> muscle energy</li>
</ul>
<p>If SAM is depleted, all of these fail simultaneously. The clinical presentation of SAM deficiency is therefore heterogeneous — affects every system that uses methylation.</p>

<h2 id="mthfr-polymorphisms">MTHFR Polymorphisms</h2>
<p>MTHFR C677T and A1298C are common variants that reduce enzyme efficiency:</p>
<ul>
<li>Homozygous C677T: ~70% reduction in MTHFR activity</li>
<li>Compound heterozygous: ~50–60% reduction</li>
<li>Prevalence: ~40–60% of people have at least one copy of C677T</li>
</ul>
<p>The clinical implication: these individuals cannot efficiently convert dietary folate to 5-MTHF, making supplemental methylfolate (5-MTHF) rather than folic acid essential for maintaining the SAM cycle.</p>
<p>Elevated homocysteine is the diagnostic indicator that the cycle is impaired. Target homocysteine: &#x3C;7 μmol/L for optimal methylation function.</p>
<h2 id="the-alternative-pathway-bhmt">The Alternative Pathway — BHMT</h2>
<p>Betaine:homocysteine methyltransferase (BHMT) operates in the liver independently of MTHFR:</p>
<pre><code>Betaine + Homocysteine → Methionine + Dimethylglycine
</code></pre>
<p>This alternative pathway:</p>
<ul>
<li>Bypasses the MTHFR step entirely</li>
<li>Is specific to liver (not active in other tissues)</li>
<li>Requires betaine (from beets, spinach, wheat germ, or TMG supplement)</li>
<li>Provides a meaningful bypass for MTHFR-impaired individuals</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="the-transsulfuration-output-glutathione">The Transsulfuration Output — Glutathione</h2>
<p>The transsulfuration pathway converts homocysteine → cystathionine → cysteine → glutathione. This means:</p>
<ol>
<li>The methylation cycle and the antioxidant defense system share a common substrate (homocysteine)</li>
<li>High methylation demand can compete with glutathione synthesis for homocysteine</li>
<li>High oxidative stress depletes glutathione → homocysteine accumulates → homocysteine-mediated vascular and epigenetic damage</li>
<li>NAC supplementation provides cysteine downstream of the homocysteine branch point — it feeds glutathione synthesis without competing with the methylation cycle for homocysteine</li>
</ol>
<p>This is the mechanistic reason why NAC + betaine/TMG together address both the antioxidant and epigenetic substrate deficits more effectively than either alone.</p>

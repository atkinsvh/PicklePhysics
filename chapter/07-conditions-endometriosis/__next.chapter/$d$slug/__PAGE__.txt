3:Te10,<h1 id="endometriosis">Endometriosis</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Retrograde menstruation is universal. Peritoneal macrophage efferocytosis failure is what separates women who develop endometriosis from those who don't.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="the-efferocytosis-failure-model">The Efferocytosis Failure Model</h2>
<p>Retrograde menstruation (endometrial cells deposited in the peritoneal cavity during menstruation) occurs in most women. Normally, peritoneal macrophages clear this tissue efficiently through efferocytosis within days.</p>
<p>In endometriosis, peritoneal macrophage efferocytosis is specifically impaired by:</p>
<ul>
<li>OxLDL and OXLAMs from dietary oxidized fat exposure → MERTK/AXL downregulation</li>
<li>Lipid-laden foam cell macrophages in the peritoneal cavity → reduced phagocytic capacity</li>
</ul>
<p>The deposited endometrial cells survive, implant, and establish lesions. The same macrophages that fail to clear them simultaneously produce:</p>
<ul>
<li>VEGF → pathological angiogenesis sustaining the ectopic tissue</li>
<li>TNF-α, IL-6, IL-8 → recruit more dysfunctional macrophages</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="local-aromatase-autocrine-loop">Local Aromatase Autocrine Loop</h2>
<p>Ectopic endometrial tissue develops local aromatase activity independent of systemic estrogen:</p>
<ul>
<li>Produces its own estradiol</li>
<li>Drives its own growth through ER→cAMP→aromatase positive feedback</li>
<li>Simultaneously loses progesterone receptor expression → progesterone resistance → loss of the normal anti-proliferative signal</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="the-iron-problem">The Iron Problem</h2>
<p>Menstrual blood deposits iron in the peritoneal cavity:</p>
<ul>
<li>Free iron → Fenton chemistry: Fe²⁺ + H₂O₂ → OH• + OH⁻ + Fe³⁺</li>
<li>Hydroxyl radical generation → pro-oxidant peritoneal environment</li>
<li>Further impairs peritoneal macrophage efferocytosis capacity</li>
<li>Creates local oxidative stress driving VEGF and inflammatory cytokine production</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="protocol-specific-additions">Protocol-Specific Additions</h2>
<p><strong>Lactoferrin (100–300mg daily):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Iron-binding glycoprotein specifically chelates free peritoneal iron</li>
<li>Reduces Fenton chemistry in the peritoneal cavity</li>
<li>Directly promotes peritoneal macrophage efferocytosis through TLR4-independent mechanisms</li>
<li>Reduces local VEGF production (the angiogenesis sustaining lesions)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Anti-angiogenic framing of the botanical pack:</strong>
The hawthorn/dan shen/pine bark OPC additions are framed elsewhere as therapeutic angiogenesis for connective tissue repair. For endometriosis, the same compounds normalize rather than simply promote angiogenesis — they suppress the pathological VEGF-driven vessel formation in lesions while maintaining normal vessel function in healthy tissue.</p>
<p><strong>DIM (from red cabbage fermentation):</strong>
Shifts estrogen metabolism toward 2-ME2 (2-methoxyestradiol) — specifically anti-angiogenic and anti-proliferative. 2-ME2 inhibits HIF-1α in endometrial cells, reducing the hypoxic signaling that drives lesion angiogenesis and growth.</p>

20:T11de,<h1 id="efferocytosis-failure">Efferocytosis Failure</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>The initiating event for autoimmune antibody production. Not a failure of immune activation — a failure of immune cleanup.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="what-efferocytosis-is">What Efferocytosis Is</h2>
<p>Every day the body produces ~200–300 billion cells undergoing programmed death (apoptosis). Under normal conditions, macrophages and dendritic cells clear these dying cells within hours through efferocytosis — phagocytic engulfment before the cells lyse. When this clearance fails, apoptotic cells undergo <strong>secondary necrosis</strong>: they rupture, releasing their nuclear contents into the extracellular space.</p>
<p>Nuclear material (DNA, histones, ribonucleoproteins, citrullinated proteins) is recognized by TLR7 and TLR9 as viral-pattern danger signals. B cells and plasmacytoid dendritic cells respond by producing autoantibodies against this nuclear material. The adaptive immune system has been educated to attack self.</p>
<h2 id="how-oxidized-lipids-impair-it">How Oxidized Lipids Impair It</h2>
<p><strong>MERTK and AXL receptor inactivation:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>These are the primary macrophage receptors for recognizing phosphatidylserine exposed on apoptotic cells</li>
<li>OxLDL and OXLAMs bind to and downregulate MERTK expression through miR-155 upregulation</li>
<li>Direct 4-HNE adduct formation impairs receptor function</li>
<li>The more oxidized lipid burden a macrophage carries, the less efficiently it performs efferocytosis</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>C1q depletion:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>C1q is the complement protein that opsonizes apoptotic cells for macrophage recognition</li>
<li>C1q is a collagen-domain protein requiring vitamin C, glycine, and lysine hydroxylation — same enzymes impaired in EDS</li>
<li>The same enzyme impairment driving connective tissue fragility also reduces complement-mediated efferocytosis</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Cholesterol efflux impairment:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Macrophages must export cholesterol to perform repeated phagocytic cycles</li>
<li>Oxidized cholesterol impairs ABCA1 and ABCG1 (cholesterol export transporters)</li>
<li>Lipid-laden macrophages become foam cells: still present but non-functional for efferocytosis</li>
<li>→ Niacin restores ABCA1 expression; EPA/DHA → resolvins directly activate efferocytosis</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="the-gas6-protein-s-vitamin-k2-connection">The Gas6/Protein S — Vitamin K2 Connection</h2>
<p>Gas6 and Protein S are the ligands for MERTK and AXL. Both are vitamin K2-dependent proteins (Gla proteins requiring carboxylation). Vitamin K2 deficiency → impaired Gas6/Protein S → impaired MERTK/AXL signaling → efferocytosis failure independent of the oxidized lipid receptor impairment.</p>
<p>MK-7 from natto is the most bioavailable dietary K2 source. This is why natto is non-optional in the protocol.</p>

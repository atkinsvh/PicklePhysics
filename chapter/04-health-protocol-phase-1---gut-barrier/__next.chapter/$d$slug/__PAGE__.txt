3:T16a3,<h1 id="phase-1-gut-barrier-establishment">Phase 1 — Gut Barrier Establishment</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Restoring the physical and functional integrity of the intestinal wall. This is both the primary therapeutic target and the primary mechanism by which everything else improves.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="why-the-gut-barrier-is-central">Why the Gut Barrier Is Central</h2>
<p>The gut barrier is not just a therapeutic target — it is the mechanism through which dietary oxidized lipids cause systemic harm. Restoring it:</p>
<ol>
<li>Stops the LPS flood into portal circulation (removing the primary systemic TLR4 activation signal)</li>
<li>Reduces OXLAM systemic distribution (they enter through the damaged barrier)</li>
<li>Restores the retinoic acid signaling that produces immune tolerance</li>
<li>Creates the environment in which the Lactobacillus culture can establish and maintain colonization</li>
<li>Reduces the estrogen recirculation driven by gram-negative beta-glucuronidase (fewer gram-negatives = less enzyme = less estrogen reabsorption)</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="interventions-weeks-1-8-concurrent-with-phase-0">Interventions — Weeks 1–8 (concurrent with Phase 0)</h2>
<h3 id="sodium-butyrate-600mg-with-each-meal">Sodium Butyrate — 600mg with each meal</h3>
<p>The direct bridge while the microbiome is being established.</p>
<p><strong>Butyrate is the master gut barrier molecule:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>HDAC inhibitor → restores H3K27 acetylation at anti-inflammatory gene loci (including FOXP3, IL-10, Nrf2 targets)</li>
<li>Upregulates tight junction proteins: occludin, claudin-3, ZO-1 — directly repairing what 4-HNE damaged</li>
<li>Primary colonocyte fuel — the energy source for maintaining the energetically expensive tight junction infrastructure</li>
<li>Mast cell stabilization (separate mechanism from tight junction)</li>
<li>GPR109a agonist → IL-18 production → epithelial repair</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Why supplement rather than just eat fiber:</strong> The butyrate-producing microbiome (Faecalibacterium prausnitzii, Roseburia, Bifidobacterium) is depleted in dysbiosis. Supplementing directly provides the signal while the culture is reestablishing.</p>
<h3 id="marshmallow-root-tea-daily">Marshmallow Root Tea — Daily</h3>
<p>Physical epithelial protection through mucilage polysaccharides:</p>
<ul>
<li>Stimulates goblet cell MUC2 production (the primary intestinal mucin — the physical barrier that allows Lactobacillus to adhere)</li>
<li>Coats gut epithelium providing physical protection during barrier repair</li>
<li>Cannot be substituted by any other intervention — physical coating is a distinct mechanism from molecular signaling</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="l-rhamnosus-gg-10-billion-cfu-daily">L. rhamnosus GG — 10 billion CFU daily</h3>
<p>Strain-specific benefits:</p>
<ul>
<li>Produces diamine oxidase (DAO) — the primary histamine-degrading enzyme</li>
<li>Specifically upregulates tight junction protein expression</li>
<li>Produces lactic acid that selectively suppresses gram-negative pathogens</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="akkermansia-muciniphila-pasteurized-form">Akkermansia muciniphila (pasteurized form)</h3>
<p>The architect of the mucin layer. Degrades and rebuilds the mucus layer, providing the substrate for Lactobacillus colonization and tight junction maintenance. Pasteurized form shown effective in clinical studies.</p>
<h3 id="fermented-preparation-introduction">Fermented Preparation Introduction</h3>
<p>Begin at 1 teaspoon daily. Increase by 1 teaspoon per week. Full dose by week 8.</p>
<p><strong>The histamine paradox and the DAO bridge:</strong> Fermented foods are simultaneously the highest dietary histamine sources AND the best path to restoring histamine degradation capacity. For histamine-sensitive individuals (MCAS):</p>
<ul>
<li>DAO enzyme supplement before EVERY serving of fermented food for first 4–6 weeks</li>
<li>Start with young ferments (days 3–5) — histamine accumulates with fermentation age</li>
<li>B6 (P5P form) + copper 2mg + vitamin C — DAO enzyme cofactors</li>
<li>→ [[Mast Cell Biology]] for full histamine management</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="oat-beta-glucan-3-5g-daily">Oat Beta-Glucan — 3–5g daily</h3>
<p>Specifically feeds Akkermansia muciniphila and induces IL-10 from gut dendritic cells. Powder form, tasteless in food or brine. Directly downregulates the Th2 skewing driving eczema pathology.</p>
<h2 id="the-retinoic-acid-connection">The Retinoic Acid Connection</h2>
<p>Intestinal epithelial cells and dendritic cells convert vitamin A to retinoic acid. Retinoic acid:</p>
<ul>
<li>Induces Treg differentiation from naive T cells in gut-associated lymphoid tissue (the primary tolerance-induction mechanism)</li>
<li>Induces gut-homing receptors on B cells (causes them to produce IgA instead of IgE)</li>
</ul>
<p>Gut epithelial damage (from oxidized lipid exposure) specifically impairs this retinoic acid signaling → tolerance machinery fails → new antigens are educated in an inflammatory rather than tolerogenic context → sensitization accumulates.</p>
<p>Restoring gut barrier integrity restores the retinoic acid signaling that produces systemic immune tolerance. This is why gut barrier restoration is upstream of mast cell, autoimmune, and hormonal interventions.</p>

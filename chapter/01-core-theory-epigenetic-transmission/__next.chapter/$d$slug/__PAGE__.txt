3:T10f2,<h1 id="epigenetic-transmission">Epigenetic Transmission</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>How the damage propagates across generations. Not genetic — epigenetic. The distinction matters because epigenetic modifications are reversible and preventable.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="the-mechanisms">The Mechanisms</h2>
<h3 id="dna-methylation-changes">DNA Methylation Changes</h3>
<p>4-HNE and MDA (malondialdehyde) modify cytosine at specific CpG sites, directly altering DNA methylation patterns at inflammatory gene promoters. The changes:</p>
<ul>
<li>Silence anti-inflammatory regulatory genes (FOXP3, IL-10)</li>
<li>Activate pro-inflammatory gene promoters persistently</li>
<li>Persist through cell division as epigenetic memory</li>
<li>Can be transmitted through gametes (sperm and egg)</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="histone-modifications">Histone Modifications</h3>
<p>4-HNE forms covalent adducts with histone proteins (H2A, H3, H4) at specific lysine and arginine residues:</p>
<ul>
<li>Disrupts electrostatic interactions compacting chromatin</li>
<li>Modifies H3K27 methylation patterns</li>
<li>Changes chromatin remodeling complex binding affinity</li>
<li>Alters transcription factor access to gene promoters</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="non-coding-rna-vectors">Non-Coding RNA Vectors</h3>
<p>miRNA, lncRNA, piRNA — the least understood but potentially most important transgenerational vector. Small RNA molecules are packaged into gametes and can transmit gene regulation patterns to offspring independent of DNA sequence.</p>
<h2 id="the-transgenerational-logic">The Transgenerational Logic</h2>
<pre><code>F0 (exposed generation): high oxidized lipid dietary exposure
        ↓ epigenetic modification
F1 (directly exposed in utero): germline epigenetic marks established
        ↓ transmitted through gametes
F2 (gestational exposure of F1 germline): second-generation effects
        ↓
F3 (first truly transgenerational generation — no direct exposure)
</code></pre>
<p>Most published studies go only to F2, which conflates direct exposure with true inheritance. F3 generation data is the rigorous standard. The generational worsening of the EDS-MCAS-eczema phenotype matches this transmission model.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-epigenetic-machinery-needs">What the Epigenetic Machinery Needs</h2>
<p>See [[One Carbon Metabolism]] and [[NAD Pool]] for the full substrate requirements.</p>
<p><strong>For DNA methylation (DNMT function):</strong>
SAM as universal methyl donor, requiring: 5-MTHF + B12 + B2 + zinc → methionine → SAM + Mg²⁺-ATP</p>
<p><strong>For active demethylation (TET enzyme function):</strong>
Alpha-ketoglutarate + Fe²⁺ (maintained by ascorbate) + O₂. Functional mitochondria are prerequisite.</p>
<p><strong>For sirtuin HDAC (NAD⁺ dependent):</strong>
NAD⁺ as consumed cosubstrate. Primary drain: PARP hyperactivation from oxidative DNA damage. Supply: NMN/NR.</p>
<h2 id="the-deadlock">The Deadlock</h2>
<p>4-HNE impairs mitochondria (Complex I/II adducts) → reduced TCA cycle → reduced alpha-ketoglutarate → TET enzymes can't demethylate → aberrant methylation persists. Meanwhile, oxidative DNA damage activates PARP → depletes NAD⁺ → sirtuins can't deacetylate → inflammatory marks accumulate. The same agent that writes the damage disables the repair machinery.</p>
<p>Breaking the deadlock requires addressing all substrates simultaneously, not sequentially. → [[Phase 3 - Epigenetic Reprogramming]]</p>
<h2 id="the-reversibility-argument">The Reversibility Argument</h2>
<p>The generational timeline is too fast for genetic drift but exactly right for epigenetic accumulation. This is evidence for reversibility — what accumulated in 2–3 generations can, in principle, be reversed in 2–3 generations of correct substrate provision and reduced exposure. The protocol addresses this at every level.</p>

20:T1ecf,<h1 id="11-step-fat-processing-protocol">11-Step Fat Processing Protocol</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>The optimized contamination reduction sequence. Two critical reorderings from the naive sequence increase stearin purity 15–20% and reduce bentonite consumption 40–50%.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="phase-structure">Phase Structure</h2>
<p><strong>Phase A (Aqueous Cleaning) — every fry session, steps 1–8</strong>
<strong>Phase B (Solvent Processing) — monthly or on QC trigger, steps 9–11</strong></p>
<h2 id="the-two-critical-reorderings">The Two Critical Reorderings</h2>
<h3 id="reordering-1-ethanol-wash-moved-from-step-10-step-6">Reordering 1: Ethanol wash moved from step 10 → step 6</h3>
<p><strong>Reason:</strong> FFAs (free fatty acids) partially co-crystallize with the stearin fraction in acetone fractionation because saturated FFAs (stearic acid mp 70°C, palmitic mp 63°C) approach crystallization conditions. Moving the ethanol wash before acetone fractionation drops FFA from ~0.78% to ~0.16% before crystallization begins — below the threshold where co-crystallization meaningfully impairs stearin purity or introduces reactive pro-oxidant FFAs into the product.</p>
<h3 id="reordering-2-bentonite-moved-from-step-7-step-10-after-acetone">Reordering 2: Bentonite moved from step 7 → step 10 (after acetone)</h3>
<p><strong>Reason:</strong> Operating on stearin fraction (~50% original volume) with dramatically reduced contamination load after acetone removes PUFA and oxidized compounds. Bentonite consumption reduced 40–50%. Each gram of bentonite has more active surface area per contaminant molecule. PUFA-associated oxidized compounds (which bentonite would otherwise have to compete with) are already gone.</p>
<p><strong>Additionally:</strong> One dry step at step 8 instead of two (before bentonite AND before acetone in original sequence) because all aqueous chemistry is now grouped before the single drying operation.</p>
<h2 id="step-by-step">Step-by-Step</h2>
<h3 id="phase-a">Phase A</h3>
<p><strong>Step 1 — Mechanical Filtration</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>100μm nylon paint strainer bag + coffee filter</li>
<li>Remove all food particles and carbonized debris</li>
<li>Particulates are primary catalysts of oxidative chain reactions</li>
<li>Contamination reduction: TPC −3%, metals −7%</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Step 2 — Baking Soda Wash</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>5g NaHCO₃/L fat dissolved in 50–100ml warm water</li>
<li>Saponifies FFAs (soap migrates to aqueous phase)</li>
<li>Degumming byproduct (phospholipid removal)</li>
<li>Self-limiting at pH 8.3 — cannot over-saponify triglycerides</li>
<li>Effervescence is a built-in FFA endpoint indicator: vigorous bubbling = high FFA; no bubbling = low FFA</li>
<li>FFA reduction: ~78%</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Step 3 — Water Wash 1</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Removes soap residue and phospholipid fragments</li>
<li>100ml water/L fat, allow phases to separate</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Step 4 — Vinegar Wash</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Dilute white vinegar to ~1% acetic acid (1:4 dilution)</li>
<li>Chelates Fe²⁺ and Cu²⁺ as metal acetates → aqueous phase</li>
<li>Breaks any residual soap from Step 2</li>
<li>Acid-activates bentonite (mild) for subsequent treatment</li>
<li>Metal reduction: ~54%</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Step 5 — Water Wash 2</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Removes acetic acid and metal acetate complexes</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Step 6 — Ethanol Wash</strong> <em>(moved earlier — critical)</em></p>
<ul>
<li>1:1 vodka (40% ethanol):fat by volume</li>
<li>Selectively dissolves residual FFAs (polar enough for ethanol, unlike triglycerides)</li>
<li>Refrigerate 30–60 min for clean phase separation</li>
<li>Discard lower ethanol-FFA phase</li>
<li>FFA reduction: ~80% of remaining</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Step 7 — Water Wash 3</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Removes residual ethanol</li>
<li>Confirm no alcohol odor before drying</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Step 8 — Dry</strong> <em>(single dry step for all aqueous chemistry)</em></p>
<ul>
<li>60–70°C with airflow, 60–90 minutes</li>
<li>Endpoint: zero bubbling</li>
<li>Verification: heat small sample to 110°C — no spattering = dry</li>
<li>Critical: residual water competes with oxidized compounds for bentonite adsorption sites in Step 10; residual ethanol dilutes acetone polarity in Step 9</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="phase-b">Phase B</h3>
<p><strong>Step 9 — Acetone Fractionation</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Ratio: 3:1 acetone:fat by volume (hardware grade, 99%+)</li>
<li>Warm to 40°C for complete dissolution</li>
<li>Counter rest at room temp 30–60 min (nucleation phase — SSS/PPP crystal seeds form)</li>
<li>Refrigerator at 4–7°C overnight (12–18 hrs) for crystal growth</li>
<li><strong>Never use freezer</strong> — produces α polymorph instead of β', crystallizes triolein (mp −4°C) into product</li>
<li>Filter through cold paint strainer bag — stearin (crystal cake) = product, olein-acetone = discard</li>
<li>PUFA reduction: −89%, TPC: −50%, peroxides: −60%</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Step 10 — Bentonite + Salt</strong> <em>(moved later — critical)</em></p>
<ul>
<li>Now on stearin fraction only (~50% original volume)</li>
<li>20–25g granular bentonite (unscented cat litter) + 15g non-iodized salt per L stearin</li>
<li>Dry shake in sealed container</li>
<li>Add hot water, shake to create emulsion</li>
<li>Refrigerate overnight — bentonite migrates to aqueous phase carrying adsorbed compounds</li>
<li>TPC: −34%, aldehydes: −40%, color: −41%</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Step 11 — Final Separation and Drying</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Warm fat to 65°C to drive off residual water</li>
<li>QC testing before storage</li>
<li>→ See [[Quality Control]]</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="crystal-chemistry-why-4-7-c-and-not-colder">Crystal Chemistry — Why 4–7°C and Not Colder</h2>
<p><strong>PUFA exclusion mechanism (geometric):</strong> Double bonds introduce ~30° bends in fatty acid chains. These geometric irregularities prevent efficient β' crystal lattice packing. PUFA triglycerides are geometrically incapable of crystallizing at 4–7°C.</p>
<p><strong>Oxidized compound exclusion (thermodynamic):</strong> Polar functional groups (−OOH, aldehyde, ketone, epoxide) form hydrogen bonds with acetone molecules. The oxidized molecule's interaction energy with acetone in solution exceeds its interaction energy with the non-polar crystal lattice. Temperature-independent within practical range.</p>
<p><strong>Why not freezer:</strong> Triolein melts at −4°C. At −18°C, triolein crystallizes into the stearin fraction. Fast cooling also produces α polymorph (wrong crystal structure) rather than β' (optimal for functional cooking fat).</p>

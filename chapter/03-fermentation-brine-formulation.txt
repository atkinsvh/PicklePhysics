<h2 id="ingredient-rationale-in-depth">Ingredient Rationale in Depth</h2>
<h3 id="glycine-8g-l">Glycine (8g/L)</h3>
<p>Glycine is the single most cross-functional addition in the entire formulation:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Glutathione synthesis:</strong> rate-limiting precursor alongside cysteine (from NAC supplementation). Neither alone is sufficient.</li>
<li><strong>Mast cell stabilization:</strong> directly activates the glycine receptor (GlyR) on mast cell surface — a chloride channel that hyperpolarizes the cell, raising the activation threshold for degranulation. This is a completely different mechanism from quercetin (calcium channel) or quercetin (histidine decarboxylase inhibition).</li>
<li><strong>Collagen:</strong> one-third of all collagen by amino acid residue. Functional deficiency in modern diets from elimination of connective tissue foods.</li>
<li><strong>One-carbon metabolism:</strong> substrate for glycine-N-methyltransferase in the SAM cycle.</li>
<li><strong>NMDA receptor co-agonist:</strong> sleep quality improvement (glycine receptor in SCN).</li>
<li><strong>Bile acid conjugation:</strong> improves fat-soluble compound absorption.</li>
</ul>
<p>At 8g/L × 13ml brine per serving × 3 meals = ~3.1g glycine daily from pickle alone. Still below full therapeutic range (3–10g) but meaningful additive contribution.</p>
<h3 id="calcium-d-glucarate-200mg-l">Calcium D-glucarate (200mg/L)</h3>
<p>Specifically inhibits beta-glucuronidase — the gram-negative bacterial enzyme that deconjugates estrogen glucuronides in the gut, enabling estrogen reabsorption and contributing to estrogen dominance. This connects gut dysbiosis to [[Estrobolome|hormonal dysregulation]] and the autoimmune amplification pathway. Not many interventions address this specific mechanism in food format.</p>
<h3 id="the-emulsification-system-lecithin-black-seed-oil-glycerol">The Emulsification System (lecithin + black seed oil + glycerol)</h3>
<p>These three work as a unit:</p>
<ul>
<li>Lecithin provides the amphiphilic structure (phospholipid heads face water, tails face oil)</li>
<li>Black seed oil provides the lipid phase into which lipophilic compounds partition</li>
<li>Glycerol bridges intermediate-polarity compounds that don't fully dissolve in either</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Preparation sequence:</strong> warm brine to 40°C → add lecithin granules → add black seed oil → add glycerol → blend vigorously 3–5 min → slight cloudiness confirms emulsion → add salt → add other powders → cool → add to vegetable-packed vessel.</p>
<h2 id="bacterial-substrates">Bacterial Substrates</h2>
<p>The molasses provides minor fermentation substrates (glucose, fructose, sucrose). The vegetable matter provides the bulk of fermentation substrate. The Lactobacillus culture does not need supplemental sugar — this is a vegetable ferment, not a sugar ferment.</p>
<h2 id="the-glycine-sam-connection">The Glycine-SAM Connection</h2>
<p>The brine is nutritionally active for the Lactobacillus culture as well as for the consumer. Lactobacillus species run their own one-carbon metabolism — the same SAM cycle producing SAH whose recycling produces AI-2 quorum sensing molecules. The glycine in the brine is simultaneously:</p>
<ol>
<li>A nutrient for the human consuming the brine</li>
<li>A potential metabolic substrate for the bacterial culture producing the fermentation compounds</li>
<li>A component of the one-carbon economy that generates the quorum sensing signals coordinating bacterial community behavior</li>
</ol>
<p>→ See [[Quorum Sensing]] for the AI-2/SAM connection</p>
<h2 id="glycine-and-the-bile-conjugation-substrate">Glycine and the Bile Conjugation Substrate</h2>
<p>The glycine in this brine is doing double duty as a fermentation substrate (the [[Quorum Sensing|AI-2 / SAM connection]]) and as a human bile acid conjugation substrate. See [[Bile-Micelle-Antioxidant Axis]] for the full layer — the modern dietary glycine deficit (~2–3g daily vs ancestral ~5–10g) creates a conjugation bottleneck that the brine's 8g/L glycine specifically addresses.</p>
<p>A 5ml daily dose of the 3-4x concentrated ferment delivers ~3g glycine from the brine fraction alone, plus additional glycine from the [[Concentration and Harvest|botanical matter]]. The combined dietary + supplement + ferment glycine load of 5-8g daily is the therapeutic range for both glutathione synthesis, mast cell GlyR stabilization, and bile acid conjugation.</p>

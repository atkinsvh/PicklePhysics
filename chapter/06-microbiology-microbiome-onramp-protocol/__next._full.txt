20:T305b,<h1 id="microbiome-onramp-protocol">Microbiome Onramp Protocol</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Vacate the niche, set the abiotic conditions, seed in the right order. Most microbiome interventions fail at step 1 — they dump strains into a defended biofilm and wonder why nothing sticks. The onramp is a 6-week structured sequence that handles all three.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="the-onramp-problem-stated-precisely">The Onramp Problem Stated Precisely</h2>
<p>You are not <em>adding</em> a community to a vacant niche. You are <strong>displacing a defended, biofilm-encased, quorum-coordinated incumbent community</strong> with one better adapted to your goals. Three sequential moves are required:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Vacate the niche</strong> — break matrix, blind QS, deny incumbent substrates</li>
<li><strong>Set abiotic conditions</strong> — pH, bile, mucin, oxygen, transit time favoring incoming community</li>
<li><strong>Seed in the right order</strong> — pioneers first, secondary wave, climax community</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="the-five-principles">The Five Principles</h2>
<p><strong>1. You cannot seed into a defended biofilm.</strong> The incumbent has matrix, QS-coordinated defense, and established substrate capture. The matrix has to be disrupted first. This is why the [[Upper GI Biofilm Disruption]] protocol precedes this one.</p>
<p><strong>2. Order matters more than dose.</strong> Pioneers modify the environment. Secondary colonizers need the modifications. Climax community needs both. Get the order wrong and you waste everything.</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Trophic level</th>
<th>Function</th>
<th>Examples</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Pioneer</td>
<td>Modifies environment</td>
<td><em>Akkermansia muciniphila</em> (mucin architect), <em>Bifidobacterium longum</em> (acidifier)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Secondary</td>
<td>Establishes core function</td>
<td><em>L. rhamnosus</em> GG, <em>L. plantarum</em>, <em>F. prausnitzii</em></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Climax</td>
<td>Diverse community</td>
<td>[[Honey Culture System]] organisms (L. kunkeei, Starmerella), wild kefir community</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p><strong>3. Substrate provision is more important than strain selection.</strong> A perfect strain in a starved environment dies. A mediocre strain in a well-fed environment thrives. The prebiotic rotation is more important than the probiotic choice.</p>
<p><strong>4. The vacate-then-feed-then-seed pattern.</strong> A 24–48 hour fast creates a transient vacant niche. The first meal after the fast should be <em>prebiotic-rich</em>, not probiotic-rich. Substrate first; culture follows.</p>
<p><strong>5. Biogeographic matching.</strong> Local raw honey, local ferments, even local soil organisms may interface with the immune system through co-evolutionary compatibility imported strains lack. The [[Honey Culture System]] "biogeographic matching principle" applies throughout.</p>
<h2 id="week-0-days-7-to-0-niche-clearing">Week 0 (Days -7 to 0): Niche Clearing</h2>
<p><strong>Daily:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>NAC 1200mg (matrix disruption)</li>
<li>Cinnamon bark decoction (1 tsp bark in 1 cup water, simmer 10 min, AM)</li>
<li>Fresh crushed garlic (2 cloves, 10 min rest, eat with food) — allicin</li>
<li>Curcumin 500mg + piperine 5mg with fatty meal</li>
<li>Nattokinase 2000 FU or serrapeptase 120,000 SPU, empty stomach</li>
<li>Bone broth 1 cup (glycine, mucin substrates, minerals)</li>
<li>L. reuteri (specific strain, e.g. ATCC PTA 6475) — produces QS-inhibiting compounds</li>
<li>L. rhamnosus GG — DAO producer, tight junction</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>No climax community introduction yet.</strong> This week is matrix breaking and substrate work only.</p>
<h2 id="week-1-days-1-7-substrate-and-acidity">Week 1 (Days 1–7): Substrate and Acidity</h2>
<p>Continue everything from Week 0. Add:</p>
<p><strong>Prebiotic rotation (rotate daily):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Day 1: PHGG (partially hydrolyzed guar gum) 5g — feeds butyrate producers</li>
<li>Day 2: Oat beta-glucan 5g — <em>Akkermansia</em> food</li>
<li>Day 3: Chicory inulin 5g (long-chain, distal colon delivery)</li>
<li>Day 4: GOS (galacto-oligosaccharides) 5g if available — <em>Bifidobacterium</em> specific</li>
<li>Day 5: Resistant starch 15g (cooked-then-cooled potato/rice) — butyrate producer fuel</li>
<li>Day 6: Acacia fiber 5g (gentle, broad)</li>
<li>Day 7: PHGG 5g (cycle)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Bile support:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>TUDCA 250–500mg with meals</li>
<li>Ox bile 125–250mg with fatty meals</li>
<li>Phosphatidylcholine 5g with meals</li>
<li>Digestive bitters (dandelion + gentian + artichoke) 15 min before largest meal</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Acidity establishment:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Raw apple cider vinegar 1 tbsp in water, 15 min before meals</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="week-2-days-8-14-pioneer-introduction">Week 2 (Days 8–14): Pioneer Introduction</h2>
<p>This is when the mucin architect comes in. Continue substrate rotation. Lower matrix breaker doses (you don't want to keep disrupting once seeding begins).</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Akkermansia muciniphila</strong> (pasteurized form) — 10^10 CFU daily, AM empty stomach. Degrades and rebuilds mucin — niche engineering. Takes 2–3 weeks to fully establish. <em>Pasteurized is the form with clinical evidence; live form has unresolved safety questions.</em></li>
<li><strong>Bifidobacterium longum</strong> — 10^10 CFU daily. Creates acidic environment. Produces folate (one-carbon substrate — see [[One Carbon Metabolism]]). Pioneer secondary colonizer.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="week-3-days-15-21-secondary-wave">Week 3 (Days 15–21): Secondary Wave</h2>
<p>The environment should now be acidic, mucin-rebuilding, substrate-rotated. Add:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>L. rhamnosus GG</strong> — 10^10 CFU daily (started in Week 0, formal colonization phase)</li>
<li><strong>L. plantarum</strong> — 10^10 CFU daily. Bile-resistant, good colonizer.</li>
<li><strong>Saccharomyces boulardii</strong> — 5 billion CFU daily. Yeast; clears residual pathogens, conditions environment. Does not take permanent residence.</li>
<li><strong>Faecalibacterium prausnitzii</strong> — 10^10 CFU daily if sourceable. Most butyrate-producing keystone; difficult to manufacture (obligate anaerobe) so most products lack it. Specialty suppliers only.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="week-4-days-22-28-diverse-community-introduction">Week 4 (Days 22–28): Diverse Community Introduction</h2>
<p>The climax community. The [[Honey Culture System]] and wild kefir come in <em>now</em>, not earlier.</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p><strong>Honey culture</strong>: 200–300ml activated culture weekly, rotating variety per the monthly schedule</p>
<ul>
<li>L. kunkeei and fructophilic LAB (climax bee-derived community)</li>
<li>Starmerella bombicola (sophorolipid production continues to disrupt pathogen biofilms)</li>
<li>Start with buckwheat (rutin-metabolizing) or local raw (biogeographic matching)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Wild kefir</strong>: ¼ cup daily, with DAO enzyme 20 min before, with fat-containing meal</p>
<ul>
<li><em>Do not start with large doses</em> — the [[Die-off Management|die-off]] and [[Phase 2 - Mast Cell Stabilization|mast cell]] load must be managed</li>
<li>1 tbsp minimum start for anyone with MCAS/EDS/histamine tendency</li>
<li>Wild kefir grains carry more diverse strains than commercial</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Begin the 40-gallon ferment brine</strong> (or oxymel/scale-down version) at ½ tsp daily</p>
<ul>
<li>Histamine load ramps gradually</li>
<li>The ferment itself delivers a community adapted to that specific botanical matrix</li>
<li>A directed ecosystem, not a generic probiotic</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="week-5-days-29-35-community-integration">Week 5 (Days 29–35): Community Integration</h2>
<p>Continue all of the above. Add:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Multi-strain rotation</strong> of secondary species (alternate L. and Bifido emphasis)</li>
<li><strong>Honey variety rotation</strong> through the monthly schedule</li>
<li><strong>Fermented food diversity</strong> beyond the ferment brine:
<ul>
<li>Raw sauerkraut (small amounts, traditionally fermented, refrigerated)</li>
<li>Kimchi</li>
<li>Traditional miso (live, not pasteurized)</li>
<li>Each adds different community members</li>
<li>All with DAO bridge</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="week-6-day-36-onward-maintenance">Week 6+ (Day 36 Onward): Maintenance</h2>
<p>This is [[Phase 1 - Gut Barrier]] of the vault's protocol in maintenance mode:</p>
<ul>
<li>Continue prebiotic rotation</li>
<li>Continue weekly honey culture</li>
<li>Continue low-dose wild kefir (¼–½ cup daily)</li>
<li>Continue daily fermented preparation</li>
<li>Drop aggressive matrix breakers (NAC at 600mg BID is now sufficient)</li>
<li>Continue bile support for at least 3 months</li>
<li>Add the full [[Phase 0 - Source Control and Redox]] → [[Phase 5 - Adipose Remodeling]] sequence as conditions allow</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="what-doesn-t-work">What Doesn't Work</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Generic multi-strain probiotic in a defended biofilm.</strong> Most are dead on arrival; the niche isn't prepared.</li>
<li><strong>"Take more fiber" without specificity.</strong> Soluble (PHGG, inulin) is right; insoluble (wheat bran) is roughage with no specific community benefit.</li>
<li><strong>Prebiotics only.</strong> Feeds incumbent too. Need to vacate first.</li>
<li><strong>Massive inoculation (FMT, large kefir doses).</strong> Herxheimer load and histamine risk.</li>
<li><strong>Long-term low-dose antimicrobials.</strong> Selects for resistance without breaking biofilm.</li>
<li><strong>Single-strain targeting.</strong> The keystone model requires the right <em>combination</em>.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="the-maintenance-layer">The Maintenance Layer</h2>
<p>After the 6-week onramp, the [[Wild Vinegars]] daily protocol is the maintenance layer that prevents dysbiosis recurrence. Raw apple cider vinegar + raw honey (oxymel) before meals, daily herbal-infused vinegar, and weekly honey culture additions keep the climax community seeded and the pH favorable. This is the sustainable layer underneath the active onramp.</p>

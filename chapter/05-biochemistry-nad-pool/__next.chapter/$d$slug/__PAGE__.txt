3:T13a5,<h1 id="nad-pool">NAD⁺ Pool</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>The consumed cosubstrate for sirtuin histone deacetylases. Chronically depleted in this clinical context through three simultaneous drains.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="why-nad-is-critical">Why NAD⁺ Is Critical</h2>
<p>Sirtuin family HDACs (SIRT1, SIRT3, SIRT6, SIRT7) remove acetyl groups from histones — specifically from pro-inflammatory gene promoters where NF-κB has placed acetylation marks. This is the deacetylation side of the chromatin remodeling work that butyrate's HDAC inhibition and pterostilbene's SIRT1 activation complement from opposite directions.</p>
<p>NAD⁺ is not a cofactor — it is a <strong>consumed cosubstrate</strong>:</p>
<pre><code>NAD⁺ + Acetyl-Histone → [SIRT] → Deacetylated-Histone + Nicotinamide (NAM) + O-acetyl-ADP-ribose
</code></pre>
<p>One molecule of NAD⁺ consumed per deacetylation event. When NAD⁺ is depleted, sirtuin activity collapses regardless of enzyme expression level.</p>
<h2 id="the-three-simultaneous-drains">The Three Simultaneous Drains</h2>
<h3 id="1-parp-hyperactivation-primary-drain">1. PARP Hyperactivation (Primary Drain)</h3>
<p>PARP1 and PARP2 are DNA repair enzymes that consume NAD⁺ massively when oxidative DNA damage is present:</p>
<ul>
<li>Single strand break → PARP activation → PAR chain synthesis from NAD⁺</li>
<li>One strand break can consume 80% of cellular NAD⁺ within minutes</li>
<li>Chronic low-level oxidative DNA damage from 4-HNE = chronic low-level PARP activation = chronic NAD⁺ drain</li>
<li>This drain cannot be outpaced by simple NAD⁺ precursor supplementation unless the oxidative DNA damage is also addressed</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Therefore:</strong> [[Phase 0 - Source Control and Redox]] reducing oxidative damage is as important for NAD⁺ restoration as NMN/NR supplementation. Sulforaphane → Nrf2 → antioxidant upregulation → reduced oxidative DNA damage → reduced PARP activation → NAD⁺ spared.</p>
<h3 id="2-cd38-upregulation-inflammatory-drain">2. CD38 Upregulation (Inflammatory Drain)</h3>
<p>CD38 is an ectoenzyme that degrades NAD⁺ to cyclic ADP-ribose and nicotinamide. Inflammation directly upregulates CD38 expression. More systemic inflammation → more CD38 activity → faster NAD⁺ degradation.</p>
<p>This creates a vicious cycle: NAD⁺ depletion → reduced sirtuin-mediated inflammatory gene deacetylation → more inflammation → more CD38 → more NAD⁺ depletion.</p>
<h3 id="3-sirtuin-consumption-functional-drain">3. Sirtuin Consumption (Functional Drain)</h3>
<p>The sirtuins themselves consume NAD⁺ during their beneficial activity. The paradox: the more effectively sirtuins deacetylate pro-inflammatory marks, the more NAD⁺ they consume. Supplementing NAD⁺ precursors is therefore essential even as sirtuins are activated.</p>
<h2 id="synthesis-pathways">Synthesis Pathways</h2>
<h3 id="preiss-handler-pathway">Preiss-Handler Pathway</h3>
<p>Nicotinic acid (niacin, vitamin B3) → NaMN → NaAD → NAD⁺
Most direct dietary route. Niacin specifically also induces ABCA1 (efferocytosis connection → [[Efferocytosis Failure]]) and has broad lipid-modifying effects.</p>
<h3 id="salvage-pathway">Salvage Pathway</h3>
<p>Nicotinamide (NAM, released by sirtuins) → NMN → NAD⁺ [requires NAMPT enzyme]
This pathway regenerates NAD⁺ from the nicotinamide released during sirtuin activity. NAMPT is the rate-limiting enzyme. NMN supplementation feeds directly into this pathway.</p>
<h3 id="de-novo-synthesis">De Novo Synthesis</h3>
<p>Tryptophan → kynurenine → quinolinic acid → NAD⁺
Requires B6, B2, iron, copper. Diverted under chronic inflammation — IDO1 activation shunts tryptophan toward kynurenine for immunosuppression rather than NAD⁺ production. Another reason inflammation reduces NAD⁺ availability.</p>
<h3 id="nmn-and-nr-supplementation">NMN and NR Supplementation</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>NR (Nicotinamide Riboside):</strong> Converts to NMN → NAD⁺. Well-absorbed orally.</li>
<li><strong>NMN (Nicotinamide Mononucleotide):</strong> More direct precursor. Debated whether it enters cells as NMN or must be converted to NR first — recent data suggests direct NMN transport via Slc12a8 transporter in some tissues.</li>
<li><strong>Dosing:</strong> 300–500mg daily. Higher doses (1g+) used in some longevity protocols.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Interaction with berberine:</strong> Berberine activates AMPK which can interfere with mTOR. Don't take simultaneously with NMN/NR — AMPK and mTOR pathways interact in ways that can partially cancel autophagy induction. Separate timing by several hours.</p>

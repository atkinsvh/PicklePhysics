3:T721,<h1 id="metabolic-syndrome">Metabolic Syndrome</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Not a disease of excess calories but of corrupted substrate signaling. The central insulin resistance is primarily molecular, not mechanical.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="the-substrate-first-model">The Substrate-First Model</h2>
<p>Conventional model: Overconsumption → adiposity → inflammation → insulin resistance</p>
<p>Substrate model: Dietary oxidized lipids → IRS-1 adduct formation → insulin resistance → compensatory hyperinsulinemia → adipogenesis → further inflammation</p>
<p>The distinction matters because it predicts:</p>
<ol>
<li>Metabolic syndrome without significant obesity (lean NAFLD, lean PCOS)</li>
<li>Persistence of metabolic dysfunction after weight loss (the adipose reservoir continues releasing OXLAMs)</li>
<li>Response to substrate change independently of caloric change</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="the-adipose-oxlam-reservoir">The Adipose OXLAM Reservoir</h2>
<p>OXLAMs accumulate in adipose tissue and continue releasing:</p>
<ul>
<li>TNF-α, IL-6 (maintaining systemic inflammatory baseline)</li>
<li>OXLAMs themselves (re-entering circulation from stored reserves)</li>
<li>Sustaining macrophage M1 polarization in adipose tissue (crown-like structures around dead adipocytes)</li>
</ul>
<p>This is why metabolic improvement lags dietary change — the reservoir is not instantly cleared even when dietary intake drops to near zero.</p>
<p>→ [[Phase 5 - Adipose Remodeling]] for the clearance protocol</p>

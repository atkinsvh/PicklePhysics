3:T123f,<h1 id="terpenoids">Terpenoids</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>The second major active chemistry class. Includes the highest fat-phase-partitioning antioxidants and the most important 5-LOX inhibitors.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="monoterpenes-c10">Monoterpenes (C10)</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Thymol</strong> (thyme): logP 3.3, radical chain termination, antimicrobial, quorum sensing inhibition</li>
<li><strong>Carvacrol</strong> (oregano, thyme): logP 3.5, similar to thymol</li>
<li><strong>Cinnamaldehyde</strong> (cinnamon): logP 1.9, TRPA1 agonist, the most potent natural quorum sensing inhibitor characterized. In volatile form — the tingle is compound-presence confirmation.</li>
<li><strong>Menthol</strong>, <strong>limonene</strong>: sensory and antimicrobial</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="diterpenes-c20">Diterpenes (C20)</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Carnosic acid</strong> (rosemary): logP ~4–5 (highest fat-phase partitioning among food antioxidants), thermally stable to 200°C, EU-approved food antioxidant (E392). The primary in-fryer antioxidant.</li>
<li><strong>Carnosol</strong> (rosemary, sage): similar profile to carnosic acid, Nrf2 activation</li>
<li><strong>Tanshinone IIA</strong> (dan shen/red sage): pro-angiogenic through eNOS activation. Fat-soluble, present in botanical matter.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="triterpenes-c30">Triterpenes (C30)</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>AKBA</strong> (acetyl-11-keto-beta-boswellic acid): logP 6–8, highly lipophilic (stays in botanical matter rather than extracting into brine — consume the matter directly), specific 5-LOX inhibitor, MMP-3/9 inhibitor. The mechanism behind frankincense in traditional medicine. Paired with myrrh (opioid receptor) for complete pain pathway coverage.</li>
<li><strong>Oleanolic acid</strong>: in olive leaves alongside oleuropein</li>
<li><strong>Ursolic acid</strong>: in rosemary, thyme — anti-inflammatory, Nrf2</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="sesquiterpenes-c15">Sesquiterpenes (C15)</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Thymoquinone</strong> (Nigella sativa): logP 1.9, potent 5-LOX + Nrf2 + mast cell compound. Partially aqueous-extractable. Stays in seeds in significant fraction — consume the seeds.</li>
<li><strong>Curcuminoids</strong> (turmeric): technically diarylheptanoids but similar solubility profile. logP 3.3, fat required for absorption. Piperine ×20 absorption. Fermentation environment (acidic, with piperine) maximizes extraction.</li>
<li><strong>Gingerols → shogaols</strong>: gingerols convert to shogaols (more potent TRPV1 agonists) in acidic fermentation environment.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="carotenoids-c40-tetraterpenes">Carotenoids (C40 tetraterpenes)</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Astaxanthin</strong>: unique among antioxidants — spans the full lipid bilayer (polar ends outside, non-polar middle). Mitochondrial membrane protection, renal tubular cell protection. Must be taken with fat. 12mg daily.</li>
<li><strong>Beta-carotene</strong>: pro-vitamin A, at high isolated doses actually pro-oxidant (ATBC trial). Obtain from food matrix.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="abietic-acid-pine-resin-diterpene-acid">Abietic Acid (pine resin, diterpene acid)</h2>
<ul>
<li>logP ~6 — extremely lipophilic, continuous partitioning into stored oil</li>
<li>The primary antioxidant in pine pitch lining ancient amphoras</li>
<li>Now understood as pinosylvin + abietic acid complex providing both antioxidant and SIRT1 effects</li>
<li>→ [[Roman Amphora System]]</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="the-paired-frankincense-myrrh-pharmacology">The Paired Frankincense-Myrrh Pharmacology</h2>
<p>Used paired across Egyptian, Greek, Roman, Islamic, Indian medicine simultaneously:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Frankincense (Boswellia)</strong>: AKBA → 5-LOX → leukotriene synthesis inhibition</li>
<li><strong>Myrrh (Commiphora)</strong>: furanosesquiterpenes → opioid receptor partial agonism + TRPV1 modulation</li>
</ul>
<p>Together they cover:</p>
<ol>
<li>5-LOX pathway (frankincense) — the pathway missed by NSAIDs</li>
<li>COX pathway — addressed by other botanical compounds</li>
<li>Opioid-mediated analgesia (myrrh) — central sensitization relevant</li>
<li>TRPV1 modulation (myrrh) — neurogenic inflammation</li>
</ol>
<p>The traditional pairing makes mechanistic sense — they are non-redundant, complementary targets.</p>

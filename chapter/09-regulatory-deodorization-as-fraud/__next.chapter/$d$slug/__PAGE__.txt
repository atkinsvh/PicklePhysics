3:T1520,<h1 id="deodorization-as-fraud">Deodorization as Fraud</h1>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Deodorization of degraded frying oil is the chemical equivalent of severing smoke detectors before setting a fire. It removes the evidence of the crime while leaving the criminal.</em></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="the-two-forms-of-fraud">The Two Forms of Fraud</h2>
<h3 id="form-1-industrial-deodorization-of-used-oil">Form 1 — Industrial Deodorization of Used Oil</h3>
<p>Commercial frying oils undergo industrial deodorization — steam distillation under vacuum at 220–270°C — at two points in their lifecycle:</p>
<p><strong>Legitimate first application:</strong> Initial refinery deodorization removes volatile compounds from freshly extracted oil. This is a manufacturing step, not quality fraud, though it does generate trans fatty acid isomers and process-derived oxidation products.</p>
<p><strong>Fraudulent second application:</strong> Deodorization applied to used, degraded commercial frying oil to restore sensory characteristics. The result:</p>
<ul>
<li>Smells fresh ✓</li>
<li>Appears fresh (lighter color) ✓</li>
<li>Tests acceptable for free fatty acid content ✓</li>
<li>Still contains full burden of non-volatile oxidized compounds (4-HNE precursors, OXLAMs, TPC) ✓</li>
</ul>
<p>The oil is then sold or re-used. Restaurant operators purchasing deodorized used oil at reduced cost may be unaware it has been degraded. The consumer receives no disclosure.</p>
<h3 id="form-2-onsite-oil-regeneration-services">Form 2 — Onsite Oil Regeneration Services</h3>
<p>A commercial market exists for onsite oil regeneration:</p>
<ul>
<li>Activated carbon treatment — removes color bodies and odorous compounds, does NOT reduce TPC</li>
<li>Antioxidant injection — suppresses rancidity detection, does not address existing oxidation products</li>
<li>Nitrogen blanketing + heat treatment — volatilizes off-flavors, non-volatile toxicants remain</li>
<li>Re-blending with fresh oil — dilutes sensory detectability, may not bring TPC below threshold</li>
</ul>
<p>These systems are sold with the explicit promise of dramatically extended oil life. Some are marketed as allowing indefinite oil use with continuous treatment.</p>
<p><strong>The distinction between legitimate and fraudulent onsite treatment is analytically precise:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Legitimate:</strong> Magnesium silicate, food-grade bentonite clay, diatomaceous earth — physically adsorb and remove polar compounds, genuinely reduce TPC. Should be encouraged.</li>
<li><strong>Fraudulent:</strong> Any treatment that restores sensory presentation without reducing TPC.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="the-statutory-framework">The Statutory Framework</h2>
<p>Section 402(a)(1) of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (21 U.S.C. § 342):
<em>"A food shall be deemed to be adulterated if it bears or contains any poisonous or deleterious substance which may render it injurious to health."</em></p>
<p>The non-volatile oxidized lipid fraction (4-HNE precursors, OXLAMs, TPC) in deodorized end-of-life frying oil:</p>
<ol>
<li>Is a "poisonous or deleterious substance" — documented in peer-reviewed literature</li>
<li>"May render it injurious to health" — the mechanisms are fully characterized</li>
<li>Has been specifically concealed from detection by the deodorization or regeneration process</li>
</ol>
<p>This meets the statutory definition of adulteration.</p>
<h2 id="the-evolutionary-sensory-argument">The Evolutionary Sensory Argument</h2>
<p>The human olfactory system can detect:</p>
<ul>
<li>Hexanal at ~4.5 ppb</li>
<li>(E)-2-nonenal at ~0.08 ppb (the "old fat" smell)</li>
<li>(E,E)-2,4-decadienal at ~0.07 ppb</li>
</ul>
<p>These detection thresholds are near the physical limits of aerosol detection — evidence of strong evolutionary selective pressure to detect these specific compounds. They evolved as precursor signals to the non-volatile toxic fraction — generated earlier in the oxidation cascade and volatile where the toxic fraction is not.</p>
<p>Deodorization neutralizes this evolved detection system with precision. It is not coincidentally masking a warning signal — it is specifically targeting the compounds the human sensory system was calibrated to detect.</p>
<p>→ [[Evolutionary Sensory Framework]] for the full sensory argument</p>
<h2 id="the-regulatory-philosophy-argument">The Regulatory Philosophy Argument</h2>
<p>Whatever else the FDA regulates, it should not permit:</p>
<ol>
<li>The targeted neutralization of evolved consumer safety mechanisms</li>
<li>Without replacing them with an equivalent analytical protection</li>
</ol>
<p>Deodorization destroys the first protection. The absence of TPC standards eliminates the second. The consumer is left with no detection mechanism for a confirmed biological hazard.</p>
<p>The [[TPC Standards|TPC standard]] is the replacement. It is immune to sensory restoration. It is the analytically correct instrument.</p>

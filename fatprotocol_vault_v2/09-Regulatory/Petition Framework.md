> *Complete structure of the regulatory petition to the Secretary of HHS. Six specific asks, organized from most fundamental to most detailed.*

## The Five-Section Structure

### Section I — The Mechanism
What happens in an industrial fryer. Four molecule classes:
1. **4-HNE and reactive aldehydes** — genotoxic, cytotoxic, enzyme-impairing (Esterbauer et al., 1991)
2. **OXLAMs** — direct TLR4 agonists, measurable at concentrations produced by commercial frying
3. **Total Polar Compounds** — the aggregate non-volatile toxic burden
4. **The food transfer mechanism** — oxidized compounds preferentially transfer into the starch matrix of fried foods

### Section II — The Fraud (Expanded)
Two forms:

**Industrial deodorization of used oil:**
- Removes volatile early-warning compounds
- Leaves TPC intact
- Makes degraded oil appear and smell fresh
- Defeats evolved consumer detection system

**Onsite oil regeneration services:**
- Activated carbon, antioxidant injection, nitrogen treatment, dilution blending
- Restores sensory presentation without reducing TPC
- Marketed explicitly for extended oil life / reduced oil change frequency
- Operates continuously at point of use
- Invisible to any oversight mechanism short of TPC measurement

**The legitimate vs. fraudulent distinction:**
- Legitimate: Magnesium silicate, bentonite clay, diatomaceous earth — genuinely reduce TPC
- Fraudulent: Any treatment restoring sensory presentation without reducing TPC

**The statutory framework:**
21 U.S.C. § 342(a)(1) — adulteration through presence of deleterious substance. Both forms of treatment meet this standard.

### Section III — The Regulatory Gap
International TPC standards vs. US absence. Germany/France: 27%. Austria/Spain/Belgium/Sweden: 25%. US: none.

### Section IV — The Economic Opportunity
[[Renewable Diesel Displacement]] argument. Converts potential opposition to stakeholders.

### Section V — Requested Actions (Six Points)
1. Federal maximum TPC standard ≤27%, mandatory disposal, universal food service application
2. Prohibition on sale/transfer/re-introduction of used oil that has undergone industrial deodorization or sensory-masking treatment — classified as adulteration under 21 U.S.C. § 342
3. Onsite commercial oil treatment system regulation — require demonstrated TPC reduction for market approval; prohibit sensory-restoration-without-TPC-reduction systems when applied above threshold oil
4. Mandatory TPC monitoring in high-volume operations; proxy cumulative-hours standards for lower-volume operations
5. GMP rulemaking for commercial fryer operation
6. FDA-commissioned independent scientific review of dietary OXLAM health effects at commercial frying concentrations

### Section VI — Conclusion
The three failures (no sensory protection, no TPC standard, no enforcement) and what the department has authority to do about each.

## The Evolutionary Sensory Addendum

A compelling supplementary argument: deodorization is the chemical equivalent of severing smoke detectors before setting a fire. The human olfactory system evolved over millions of years specifically to detect oxidized fat before consumption. No regulatory framework should permit the targeted neutralization of evolved consumer safety mechanisms without requiring equivalent replacement protection.

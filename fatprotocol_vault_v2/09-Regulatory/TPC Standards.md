# TPC Standards

> *Total Polar Compounds is the only fraud-resistant regulatory instrument for frying oil quality. Every other measure can be defeated by deodorization or sensory restoration.*

## Why TPC Is the Right Measure

Total Polar Compounds is the aggregate measure of all non-volatile oxidation products in frying oil:
- Lipid hydroperoxides and their decomposition products
- Aldehydes, ketones, epoxides
- Polar polymers and dimers from triglyceride oxidation
- Free fatty acids
- Everything that shouldn't be there after heating

**The critical property: TPC cannot be reduced by any sensory-restoration treatment.**
- Deodorization removes volatile early-warning compounds — TPC unchanged
- Activated carbon removes color bodies — TPC unchanged
- Antioxidant injection suppresses rancidity detection — TPC unchanged
- Dilution with fresh oil reduces TPC proportionally — detectable by any retest

TPC is the only measure that requires the actual contaminants to be removed rather than masked.

## International Standards

| Country | Maximum TPC | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|
| Germany | 27% | Mandatory disposal, food safety inspection |
| France | 27% | Mandatory disposal, penalties |
| Austria | 25% | Mandatory disposal |
| Spain | 25% | Mandatory disposal |
| Belgium | 25% | Mandatory disposal |
| Sweden | 25% | Mandatory disposal |
| **United States** | **No federal standard** | **No enforcement** |

European inspectors test TPC directly. Oil above threshold is confiscated. Operators are fined. The standard cannot be satisfied by sensory restoration — TPC is measured directly, not inferred from appearance or smell.

## US Regulatory Gap

In the United States, a commercial fryer operator may legally serve food fried in oil at 40%, 50%, or 60% TPC — oil that would be confiscated in Frankfurt, Paris, or Vienna — with:
- No legal consequence
- No obligation of disclosure
- No regulatory framework governing the practice

An onsite regeneration service may restore the sensory presentation of 60% TPC oil to apparent freshness and sell a subscription contract for continuous treatment, and no regulatory provision governs this practice whatsoever.

## Measurement Methods

**Professional (laboratory):**
- AOCS Official Method Cd 22-91 (chromatographic TPC measurement)
- Dielectric constant measurement (portable instruments)

**Commercial portable instruments:**
- FOM (Fat Oil Monitor) — dielectric constant, quick-read
- Testo 270 — same principle
- Used by European health inspectors in the field
- ~$300–500 USD, very accurate

**The effervescence test (proxy, free):**
As described in [[Aqueous Phase Chemistry]] — baking soda effervescence is proportional to FFA content, which correlates with overall TPC. Not precise enough for regulatory compliance but adequate for operational monitoring.

## The Correct Standard for a US Petition

**The petition asks for:** Maximum 27% TPC (the more permissive European standard)
- Starting with 27% rather than 25% is politically pragmatic — harder to argue it's too strict when Germany and France have used it for decades
- Once established, tightening to 25% in a subsequent rulemaking is straightforward

**With explicit provision that TPC measurement cannot be substituted by sensory assessment** — this forecloses the industry argument that regular sensory checks are equivalent.

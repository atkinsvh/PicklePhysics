# Sensory QC Protocol

> *Using the evolved sensory apparatus as an analytical instrument. Establish a reference profile. Compare each batch against it. Deviation indicates chemistry change.*

## The Reference Batch System

For any reliable sensory QC, a reference standard is required:

**For fat processing:**
- Keep a sealed jar of freshly processed stearin immediately after completion of the 11-step protocol
- This is your reference for: color, odor at 70°C, water drop surface tension behavior
- Label with date and FFA titration result
- Replace every 6 months
- Photograph each QC comparison for trend tracking

**For fermented preparation:**
- After a batch is verified by pH measurement, taste assessment, and trigeminal profile
- Take a sealed small jar as the reference
- Refrigerate; replace with each new batch
- All subsequent batches compared against this reference

## Fat Quality Assessment

### Color (visual)
- Reference: ivory to pale yellow, translucent when melted
- Early degradation: amber tint
- Significant degradation: orange-amber
- Severe degradation: dark amber to brown
- **The brownness is not random — it is specifically Maillard-type polymeric oxidation products, the same compounds the bentonite column adsorbs**

### Odor at 70°C (olfactory)
Warm a small sample to exactly 70°C in a covered vessel, then uncover and smell:
- Reference: neutral, mildly fatty, clean
- Early degradation: faintly grassy or painty (hexanal)
- Moderate degradation: "old fat" note ((E)-2-nonenal)
- Severe degradation: rancid, harsh, deep-fried staleness

### Water Drop Test (FFA proxy)
- Place fat drop on clean dry glass at room temperature
- Add water drop
- Reference: discrete water bead, maintains surface tension, does not spread
- High FFA: water spreads and partially emulsifies (FFAs as endogenous emulsifiers)

### Paper Towel Test (combined)
- Dip white paper towel in fat at 60°C
- Reference: even pale yellow absorption, no dark patches
- Degraded: dark patches, uneven absorption
- Run after each bentonite treatment — endpoint when no further improvement visible

## Fermented Preparation Assessment

### Color Assessment
| Color observation | Significance |
|---|---|
| Deep purple-red | Anthocyanins from red onion, red cabbage, tart cherry well-extracted |
| Orange-yellow tint in brine | Curcumin extracted and distributed in oil phase (emulsification working) |
| Clear to slightly turbid | Normal — protein hydrolysis and bacterial biomass |
| Muddy grey-brown | Investigate for putrefaction — different from the amber-brown of normal mature ferment |
| Fading purple over time | Anthocyanin degradation — check pH (more acidic = better stability) |

### Aroma Assessment (olfactory)
**Healthy:**
- Dominant clean lactic sour
- Secondary diacetyl cream note
- Botanical complexity present and identifiable (garlic, ginger, thyme)
- Mild earthy-indole in mature batches (acceptable)

**Investigate:**
- Any putrescine/cadaverine character (rotting flesh)
- Dominant H₂S (rotten egg)
- Strongly alcoholic
- Sharp pure acetic without lactic character

### Taste Assessment (gustation)
**Healthy batch profile:**
- Pleasantly sour (pH confirmation)
- Complex bitterness (active compound presence)
- Umami depth increasing with age (protein hydrolysis)
- Mild salt (osmotic environment)

**Investigate:**
- No bitterness (active compound degradation)
- No umami (fermentation stalled — protein not being hydrolyzed)
- Metallic taste (mineral imbalance or vessel corrosion)
- Soapy (high FFA from degraded botanical fats)

### Trigeminal Assessment
Run systematically through each compound-specific sensation:

| Sensation | Compound confirmed | Action if absent |
|---|---|---|
| Garlic bite | Allicin | Check garlic quantity, freshness |
| Ginger warmth | Gingerols/shogaols | Check ginger quantity |
| Pepper heat | Piperine | Ensure peppercorns are being consumed |
| Cinnamon tingle | Cinnamaldehyde | Check cinnamon bark freshness |
| Nigella warmth/flavor | Thymoquinone | Check nigella seed freshness, quantity |
| Bitter baseline | Quercetin, EGCG, thymoquinone, andrographolide | Check extraction conditions, botanical freshness |

## Developing Individual Sensory Reference

The sensory system is trainable. Over repeated assessments:
- Specific off-notes become recognizable earlier
- Compound-specific trigeminal signatures become distinguishable
- Batch-to-batch variation pattern becomes apparent

The goal is developing the capacity to assess a batch in 2–3 minutes and identify any significant departure from the established profile — a skill that comes with 5–10 systematic assessments using the framework above.

# Brine Formulation

## Ingredient Rationale in Depth

### Glycine (8g/L)
Glycine is the single most cross-functional addition in the entire formulation:
- **Glutathione synthesis:** rate-limiting precursor alongside cysteine (from NAC supplementation). Neither alone is sufficient.
- **Mast cell stabilization:** directly activates the glycine receptor (GlyR) on mast cell surface — a chloride channel that hyperpolarizes the cell, raising the activation threshold for degranulation. This is a completely different mechanism from quercetin (calcium channel) or quercetin (histidine decarboxylase inhibition).
- **Collagen:** one-third of all collagen by amino acid residue. Functional deficiency in modern diets from elimination of connective tissue foods.
- **One-carbon metabolism:** substrate for glycine-N-methyltransferase in the SAM cycle.
- **NMDA receptor co-agonist:** sleep quality improvement (glycine receptor in SCN).
- **Bile acid conjugation:** improves fat-soluble compound absorption.

At 8g/L × 13ml brine per serving × 3 meals = ~3.1g glycine daily from pickle alone. Still below full therapeutic range (3–10g) but meaningful additive contribution.

### Calcium D-glucarate (200mg/L)
Specifically inhibits beta-glucuronidase — the gram-negative bacterial enzyme that deconjugates estrogen glucuronides in the gut, enabling estrogen reabsorption and contributing to estrogen dominance. This connects gut dysbiosis to [[Estrobolome|hormonal dysregulation]] and the autoimmune amplification pathway. Not many interventions address this specific mechanism in food format.

### The Emulsification System (lecithin + black seed oil + glycerol)
These three work as a unit:
- Lecithin provides the amphiphilic structure (phospholipid heads face water, tails face oil)
- Black seed oil provides the lipid phase into which lipophilic compounds partition
- Glycerol bridges intermediate-polarity compounds that don't fully dissolve in either

**Preparation sequence:** warm brine to 40°C → add lecithin granules → add black seed oil → add glycerol → blend vigorously 3–5 min → slight cloudiness confirms emulsion → add salt → add other powders → cool → add to vegetable-packed vessel.

## Bacterial Substrates

The molasses provides minor fermentation substrates (glucose, fructose, sucrose). The vegetable matter provides the bulk of fermentation substrate. The Lactobacillus culture does not need supplemental sugar — this is a vegetable ferment, not a sugar ferment.

## The Glycine-SAM Connection

The brine is nutritionally active for the Lactobacillus culture as well as for the consumer. Lactobacillus species run their own one-carbon metabolism — the same SAM cycle producing SAH whose recycling produces AI-2 quorum sensing molecules. The glycine in the brine is simultaneously:
1. A nutrient for the human consuming the brine
2. A potential metabolic substrate for the bacterial culture producing the fermentation compounds
3. A component of the one-carbon economy that generates the quorum sensing signals coordinating bacterial community behavior

→ See [[Quorum Sensing]] for the AI-2/SAM connection

## Glycine and the Bile Conjugation Substrate

The glycine in this brine is doing double duty as a fermentation substrate (the [[Quorum Sensing|AI-2 / SAM connection]]) and as a human bile acid conjugation substrate. See [[Bile-Micelle-Antioxidant Axis]] for the full layer — the modern dietary glycine deficit (~2–3g daily vs ancestral ~5–10g) creates a conjugation bottleneck that the brine's 8g/L glycine specifically addresses.

A 5ml daily dose of the 3-4x concentrated ferment delivers ~3g glycine from the brine fraction alone, plus additional glycine from the [[Concentration and Harvest|botanical matter]]. The combined dietary + supplement + ferment glycine load of 5-8g daily is the therapeutic range for both glutathione synthesis, mast cell GlyR stabilization, and bile acid conjugation.

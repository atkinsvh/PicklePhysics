# Quality Control — Fat Processing

> *Testing hierarchy from free sensory checks to quantitative titration. The reference sample is the most important tool.*

## Testing Hierarchy

| Test | Frequency | Time | Cost | Measures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sensory (color, smell) | Every session | 2 min | Free | Overall condition proxy |
| Paper towel test | Every session | 5 min | Free | Polar compounds, particulates |
| Water drop test | Weekly | 2 min | Free | FFA proxy |
| Baking soda effervescence | Weekly/incoming | 5 min | Free | FFA (qualitative) |
| FFA titration | Monthly/post-processing | 15 min | <$1 | FFA% (quantitative) |
| Starch-iodide peroxide | Quarterly | 20 min | $2 | Peroxide value (semi-quant) |
| Color comparison | Monthly | 5 min | Free | Color body accumulation trend |

## The Reference Sample System

Keep a sealed small jar of freshly processed stearin as a permanent standard:
- Best available quality, freshly processed
- Store dark, cool, sealed
- Replace every 6 months
- Use for: color comparison, odor comparison, water drop comparison
- Photograph each test for trend tracking

**Reference standard specs:**
- FFA: <0.3%
- Color: ivory-white
- Odor (warm): neutral, mildly fatty
- Water drop: discrete bead maintained

## FFA Titration — Full Procedure

**Materials:** IPA (99%), phenolphthalein indicator, 0.1M NaOH (4g food-grade NaOH per liter distilled water), scale (±0.1g), syringe or dropper

1. Weigh 5.0g melted fat exactly
2. Add 10ml IPA, warm to 50°C until clear
3. Add 3 drops phenolphthalein (solution colorless)
4. Add 0.1M NaOH drop by drop, swirling between drops
5. Endpoint: first persistent pink lasting 30 seconds

**Calculation:** FFA% = (ml NaOH × 0.1 × 282 × 100) ÷ (sample weight × 1000)

**Example:** 0.5ml NaOH, 5.0g sample → FFA = (0.5 × 0.1 × 282 × 100) ÷ (5000) = 0.28%

**Acceptance criteria:**
- <0.5%: Release — meets specification
- 0.5–1.0%: Acceptable — monitor
- >1.0%: Reprocess — additional ethanol wash + repeat Phase A

## Sensory Tests

**Paper towel test:**
Dip white paper towel in fat at 60°C. Clean fat = even pale yellow absorption, no dark patches. Degraded fat = dark patches, uneven absorption, visible particulate residue. Run after each bentonite treatment — endpoint when successive treatments produce no improvement.

**Water drop test (FFA proxy):**
Place fat drop on clean dry glass. Add water drop. Pass = discrete water bead maintains surface tension. Fail = water spreads and partially emulsifies (FFAs acting as emulsifiers at fat-water interface).

**Baking soda effervescence:**
Add 5ml baking soda solution to 50ml fat sample. Vigorous sustained bubbling = high FFA. Mild brief = moderate. None = low FFA. Use as incoming quality assessment and post-processing check.

## Bentonite Saturation Indicator

Fresh bentonite: pale tan to grey-white. Spent bentonite: darkens toward amber-brown proportional to oxidized compound load.

- Spent bentonite exits already dark → fat had high contamination load, continue treatment
- Spent bentonite similar to fresh → fat was relatively clean, treatment is complete

**Diminishing returns endpoint:** Plot FFA or color measurement after each treatment. Continue until the improvement curve flattens — where another treatment produces no measurable improvement. This is both the quality endpoint and the economic optimization point.
